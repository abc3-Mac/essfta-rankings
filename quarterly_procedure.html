Quarterly Rankings Procedure

How the quarterly rankings are calculated from official AKC data · September 2026 · rules confirmed with Polly Mumma and Betsy Baird · updated September 16 (S-line and tie conventions)

This page describes, start to finish, how a quarterly ranking report is produced. The front section is the procedure in plain words. The appendices give the precise rule for every award, a guide to reading the AKC report files, and the record of how these rules were verified.

What you need

  • The AKC Quarterly Electronic Reports — one spreadsheet per sport (Obedience, Rally, Agility, Conformation, Juniors), sent by the AKC each quarter. Each file covers only its own three months. A January-through-June report therefore needs both the 1st-quarter and 2nd-quarter files; the year-end report needs all four.
  • The New Titles file — the AKC's monthly list of every title earned by every Springer, kept as a running master file. This is how you know which year a dog earned its title.
  • The ESSFTA membership list — used only to mark members with an asterisk. Membership never decides who appears in the quarterly standings.

Ties, in every sport: tied dogs are listed alphabetically by registered name (championship prefixes ignored) and share a rank number; the next rank skips accordingly — 1, 1, 1, 4. Alphabetical order keeps tie listings above any suspicion of favoritism.

Class order on the card: regular titling classes come first, then the optional ones — in rally, Master is listed before Intermediate, which is an optional titling class; obedience follows the scorecard's order (Novice, Open, Utility, Combined, Preferred, then Beginner Novice and Graduate).

The procedure

  1. Gather the quarter's files. Collect every sport's report for every quarter of the year so far. Do not assume one file holds the whole year — it never does.
  2. Load them into the Rankings Calculator. Open the calculator page and drop all the files in at once. It recognizes each sport automatically, combines the quarters, applies the rules in Appendix A, and marks members with an asterisk. The files never leave the computer.
  3. Read the draft it produces. The calculator shows every class ranking, High Combined and High Triple for rally, the combined award for obedience, and the agility awards, each with the rule it applied printed beside it.
  4. Compare against the compiler's worksheet. Where the two disagree, the difference is always one of two things: a data question (a typo, a missed line, a stale download) or a rule question (a nuance the calculator does not know yet). The AKC report is the club's official source, so check the disputed dog's actual lines in the AKC file — they settle most differences in a minute. Rule questions go to the sport's governor.
  5. Publish the agreed report. Once the worksheet and the calculator tell the same story, the report is ready for members. The calculator exports it as a Word document or a PDF in either portrait or landscape.

The rules in one breath

Obedience. A dog appears in a class once it has three qualifying scores there, and it is ranked on the average of its three highest. Only real runs count: a specialty line marked S is the same run recorded in a second judge's book, and trophy lines carry no score of their own — neither is ever counted. A dog is listed in a titling class only in the year it earns that title, with all three legs earned that year. The Combined Open B/Utility award adds a dog's best Open B and best Utility score from the same trial and averages the five best totals; a dog needs five such trials to be listed.

Rally. The same three-highest rule, and the same reading of the specialty S lines: one run, one score — never counted twice (club decision, September 16, 2026). High Combined (Advanced B + Excellent B) and High Triple (those plus Master) pair a day's scores class by class, best with best, and average the three best combined totals.

Agility. The ESS of the Year award needs at least ten double-Qs (qualifying in both Excellent B Standard and Jumpers the same day) and ranks by the average speed of the fastest ten. Novice and Open rank on the average of the first six qualifying runs — the three Standard and three Jumpers legs that earn the title. Excellent A ranks on average score, with at least three Standard and three Jumpers scores required.

Conformation. Best of Breed points are one point per dog defeated at the show; Best of Opposite Sex adds the Best of Winners credit when it applies; Group and Best in Show points are dogs defeated in the group and in the show. The club's own database produces this report — the calculator reproduces it for verification.

How many scores it takes

Three qualifying scores is the rule everywhere in the obedience and rally classes: three to be listed, ranked on the average of the best three. It changes in five places.

AwardWhat it takes
Obedience and rally classesThree qualifying scores; average of the best three
Obedience Combined Open B/UtilityFive trials with both an Open B and a Utility score; average of the five highest totals
Rally High Combined and High TripleThree days on which the dog qualified in every class the award requires; average of the best three day totals (the year-end award uses the best five)
Agility Springer of the YearTen double-Qs in Excellent B; average speed of the fastest ten
Agility Novice and OpenThree Standard and three Jumpers legs — the six that earn the title — averaged
Agility Excellent AThree Standard and three Jumpers scores to be eligible; then all qualifying scores are averaged

Two awards have no minimum at all: the year-end Preferred agility award simply totals every Preferred qualifying run, and juniors and conformation are not score-based, so every handler or dog with a result appears.

Why compute from the raw AKC data at all? Because it verifies both directions. In the 2026 mid-year review, computing from the raw files caught a carried-forward typo, a missing dog, and a spreadsheet that had summed 98 + 95 as 196 — and, in the other direction, the compilers' worksheets caught rules the computer did not know. The finished cards are now confirmed to the last decimal.

Appendix A

The precise rules, award by award

Obedience

  • Class rankings. For every class: at least three qualifying scores in the calendar year; rank by the average of the three highest. Classes keep A and B separate. Placements are shown but add no points.
  • What counts as a qualifying score. A line whose placement is 1–4 or a plain qualifying mark. Lines with an S placement are the same run recorded in a concurrent specialty's judge's book — two placements, two sets of prizes, one score — and are never counted. Trophy lines (High in Trial, High Combined, and similar) repeat scores already counted, or carry combined totals that are not run scores; they are never counted.
  • Titling classes and the calendar. A dog appears in a titling class (Novice A/B, Open A, Utility A, Beginner Novice A/B, the Preferred classes) only in the year its title is dated, and only when all three title legs fall in that year. A dog that finished its title using a leg from a previous year is not listed, even if the title certificate is dated this year. The New Titles file is the reference for title dates; previous years' titles show in the AKC's earlier title reports.
  • Combined Open B/Utility. For each trial where the dog qualified in both Open B and Utility (A or B), add the two scores. Rank by the average of the five highest totals; a minimum of five combined trials is required to be listed. The AKC report's own "combined" award lines carry no scores — the totals must be computed from the two class scores.

Rally

  • Class rankings. Three qualifying scores to appear; rank by the average of the three highest. Choice is ranked like any other class.
  • The specialty lines. S1/S2/S3 lines are the same run recorded a second time in a concurrent specialty's judge's book and are never counted — one run, one score, exactly as in obedience (club decision, September 16, 2026). Award lines (HTQ, HIGHCM, HCS, HTQS) hold combined totals, not run scores, and are never counted either.
  • High Combined and High Triple. High Combined pairs Advanced B with Excellent B; High Triple adds Master. For each day (event and date), pair the dog's scores class by class — on a day with two scores per class, the best pairs with the best and the second with the second, giving two combined totals. Rank by the average of the three highest totals; at least three totals to be listed. The year-end award uses the five highest instead.

Agility

  • ESS of the Year. At least ten double-Qs in Excellent B; rank by the average yards-per-second of the fastest ten. A double-Q is qualifying in both Standard and Jumpers on the same day; its speed is the mean of the two runs (speed = course distance ÷ dog's time).
  • Novice and Open. Average of the first six qualifying runs: the three Standard and three Jumpers legs that earn the title.
  • Excellent A. Average of the qualifying scores, with an eligibility requirement of at least three Standard and three Jumpers scores.
  • Preferred. The quarterly sheet ranks Preferred ESS of the Year by the fastest-ten double-Q speed in the Preferred classes; the year-end award totals all Preferred qualifying runs, ties broken by speed. Preferred class awards average across both preferred classes' legs together.

Conformation

  • Best of Breed points — one point for every dog and bitch defeated at the show, classes included.
  • Best of Opposite Sex points — one point per same-sex dog defeated, plus, when Best of Winners went to the same sex as the BOS winner, one point for every dog shown in the classes.
  • Group and Best in Show — one point per dog defeated in the group or in the show; on a Best in Show day the group points fold into the BIS points, with no double counting.
  • Owner-handled and puppy series are separate programs and are not counted. The "Spec" columns count specialties and the National, plus designated specialties — a springer club's specialty held at an all-breed show. The AKC results file lists those as ordinary all-breed shows, so their event numbers come from the AKC event search (Specialties (S/PS/DS), English Springer Spaniel, competition type DS).

Appendix B

Reading the AKC report files

Every sport's file has one row per recorded result, keyed by AKC registration number — the registration number, not the dog's name, is the dog's identity, because names grow new title letters through the year.

Placement codeWhat it isObedienceRally
QLFY, 1–4 A real qualifying runcountscounts
S1, S2, S3 The same run recorded in a concurrent specialty's judge's book never countsnever counts (club decision, Sept 2026)
HIGHT, HIGHCM, HTS, HTP, HTQ, HTQS, HCS Trophy and award markers — duplicates or combined totals never countsnever counts

Things the files do that are easy to trip over:

  • Each file is one quarter only. The published reports are year-to-date, so every quarter's file must be loaded together.
  • Dog names contain invisible non-breaking spaces and accumulate title letters as the year goes on. Match dogs by registration number.
  • Two-trial days. At ordinary clubs, both trials' runs appear as ordinary rows. The S lines appear only at Springer specialty events.
  • Hand-tallying hazard. When day scores are copied into a worksheet, a two-trial day invites the classic slip of pairing or adding the wrong two numbers. The 2026 mid-year review found exactly one of these (98 + 95 recorded as 196), which is a good argument for letting the calculator do the addition.

Appendix C

How these rules were verified

The rules above are not guesses; each was tested by computing the rankings from the raw AKC files and comparing against the finished reports, then resolving every difference with the sport's governor.

  • Q1 2026: the computed rankings reproduced the published reports to the decimal for all three companion sports.
  • Jan–Jun 2026 Agility: perfect reconciliation with the compiled stats sheet — every award, every value, same order.
  • Jan–Jun 2026 Conformation: every rank, count, and points cell reproduced exactly against the club's database output (805 cells); with the designated-specialty list the specialty counts match too, and Jan–Sep 2026 matched Alvin Eng's tabulation on all 474 cells.
  • Jan–Jun 2026 Rally: the card and the calculator first reconciled on all 91 values, which is what exposed the specialty lines as double recordings; the corrected card applies the one-run-one-score rule adopted on September 16.
  • Jan–Jun 2026 Obedience: the corrected scorecard and the calculator agree on every value but three cells still under review; the method itself — three highest, specialty lines excluded, combined minimum of five — is confirmed by the compiler.
  • Titles: every dog listed in a titling class was checked against the AKC New Titles file; all thirteen title dates fall inside the ranking window.

The one open policy item was settled on September 16, 2026: rally now reads the specialty S lines exactly as obedience does — one run, one score. Both sports use the same rule, chosen deliberately.
