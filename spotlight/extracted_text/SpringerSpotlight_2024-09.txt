We send our own fear, anger, delight and physical reactions straight down the leash . Scientists
share more and more research observations about dogs’ reactions to our facial expressions, tone of
voice, our smell, and which illnesses we suffer to overcome.
In her ‘Scientific American’ article, “When Dogs Smell Your Stress, They Act Sad”, writer Sarah No-
vak finds, “Humans and dogs have been close companions for perhaps 30,000 years, according to
anthropological and DNA evidence. They have evolved to read verbal and visual cues from their owners,
and previous research has shown that with their acute sense of smell, they can even detect the odor of
stress in human sweat. Now researchers have found that not only can dogs smell stress—in this case
represented by higher levels of the hormone cortisol—they also react to it emotionally.” Novak shares
research led by Zoe Parr-Cortes (Bristol Veterinary School; University of Bristol). “For thousands of
years, dogs have learned to live with us, and a lot of their evolution has been alongside us. Both humans
and dogs are social animals, and there’s an emotional contagion between us,” says Parr-Cortes. “Being
able to sense stress from another member of the pack was likely beneficial because it alerted them of a
threat that another member of the group had already detected.”
Science writer, Russell McClendon, for Yahoo News, shares Nikola Rooney’s findings that,
"Working dog handlers often describe stress traveling down the lead, but we've shown it can also travel
through the air. Understanding how human stress affects dogs' well-being is an important consideration
for dogs in kennels," says Rooney, "and when training companion dogs and dogs for working roles such as
assistance dogs." McClendon’s article “Dogs Can Smell Our Stress, And It Affects Them Deeply” reports
many of the findings of the Bristol study in the publication, ‘Scientific Reports.’ Emotional and physical

Prioritize your health for the sake of theirs.
Research has proven this phenomenon of pets serving as mirrors ‒ reflecting not only our emotional
states, but even our physical health conditions. By recognizing the interconnected nature of the well-
being of our furry friends and ourselves, and prioritizing immune support for both, we can strengthen the
bond between humans and our loyal companions and engineer better health for all.”
Dr. Stanley Coren, ‘Psychology Today’ magazine, shares reasons for this two-way exchange, saying,
“we now understand that dogs have all of the same brain structures that produce emotions in humans.
Dogs also have the same hormones and undergo the same chemical changes that humans do during emo-
tional states. Dogs even have the hormone oxytocin, which, in humans, is involved with feeling love and
affection for others. This conclusion holds for most mental abilities, including emotions.” Coren con-
cludes, “This means that a dog will have all of the basic emotions: joy, fear, anger, disgust, and even love.
However, based on current research it seems likely that your dog will not have those more complex
emotions like guilt, pride, and shame.”
Just as dog handlers, dog trainers and all of us who interact with dogs have our own preferences for
how we learn and what kind of rewards for our behaviors affect us most, dogs, too, have multi-faceted
reactions and feelings. In his ‘National Geographic’ article, ““Dogs Have Feelings—Here's How We
Know”, writer Simon Worrall, shares the work of Emory University neuroscientist, Gregory Berns.

English Springer Foundation Bulletin
Genetics Study on IMHA in English Springer Spaniels: How You Can Help
We learn too often about another English Springer victim of IMHA, and the Foundation now includes IMHA among
its research priorities. At this year’s National Specialty, we distributed this bulletin; we want to share it with all
members of the ESSFTA, particularly those of you who may be experiencing this with one of your own beloved dogs.
The English Springer Foundation is supporting a new study to understand the genetic basis of immune-mediated
hemolytic anemia (IMHA) in English Springer Spaniels. Dr. Steven Friedenberg, University of Minnesota, is working
with his long-time collaborator Dr. Leigh Anne Clark at the University of Georgia on this project.
The Foundation strongly encourages the Springer community to participate; read on for details from Dr. Frieden-
berg about the study and how to get your dog enrolled!
What is the purpose of this study?
The purpose of this study is to identify genetic variants that are associated with IMHA in English Springer Spaniels.
We hope to develop a genetic test that can predict which dogs are at risk for developing IMHA.
What are the enrollment criteria?
Your dog must be an English Springer Spaniel to enroll in this study. Affected dogs must have either a new or his-
torical diagnosis of IMHA that we can verify by reviewing your dog’s medical records. Specifically, we will look for:
• Evidence of anemia
• Evidence of at least one immune-mediated process
• Evidence of hemolysis (at least one)
 Elevated bilirubin in the blood
 Free hemoglobin in the blood or urine
 Presence of erythrocyte ghost cells

Unaffected dogs must be at least 9 years of age and have no history of IMHA or any other autoimmune disease.
What is the process for enrolling my dog?
If your dog qualifies for the study, we will work with your regular veterinarian to arrange a small, one-time
blood draw on your dog.
If your dog has been diagnosed with IMHA in the past 24 hours, we will ask your veterinarian to draw two
blood samples, each about a teaspoon. If your dog has been diagnosed with IMHA more than 24 hours
ago, or if you are enrolling your dog as a healthy volunteer, we will only need one blood sample, also
about a teaspoon.
Your vet will then send the blood sample to our lab. We will also ask you to fill out an online survey and study
consent form so that we can collect additional information about your dog.
Are there any costs for me to enroll my dog?
There are no costs for you to participate in the study. The study will pay for the costs of shipping the blood sam-
ple to our lab and for processing the blood samples. Because this blood draw is for research purposes, most vet-
erinarians do not charge for the blood draw, but if this is required, we will work with your veterinarian to pay for
this expense.
If your dog has a new diagnosis of IMHA, we will perform red blood cell flow cytometry in our lab for free and
send the results of this test to your dog’s veterinarian. This test helps confirm the diagnosis of IMHA and may
help your veterinarian manage your dog’s disease.
Can I enroll my dog if I live outside the USA?
Yes! We can enroll dogs from around the world, but the samples we collect may be different from those we col-
lect from the USA because of shipping requirements and other country-specific restrictions. Please contact us at
imha@umn.edu for more details.
What should I do if I want to get involved?
The easiest way to get involved is to email us at imha@umn.edu. When you email us, please let us know:
• Your name
• Your dog’s name, breed, age, sex, and whether or not they are spayed/neutered
• Whether your dog has IMHA or whether you are enrolling your dog as a healthy volunteer
If your dog has IMHA, please let us know if they were diagnosed recently (past 24 hours) or historically

Report of the Conformation and Performance Bench Governors
September 7, 2024
Judges Ed, Illustrated Standard and Breeders Ed Update
We continue to make great progress on key projects in our strategic plan. Judges’ Ed was
offered on Friday, September 27 at the National Specialty following Bitch judging. Over 35
people signed up, most of whom are interested members of the fancy, as well as a number of
judge candidates. Breeders’ Ed is offered a unique opportunity on Thursday, after Dog judging,
“Finding Your Balance” to give hands on instruction on how to correctly measure your dog.
Progress on the Illustrated Standard continues, albeit at a slower pace than planned. We
presented the work completed to date during the National Specialty.
As we look at the next key initiative, a task force on Ringside Mentoring will be formed to better
define the requirements to be a ringside mentor and develop training. Ringside mentors are an
invaluable resource, and we want to be sure we are fully supporting their efforts!
General Updates
Many thanks to all the individuals who provided content for the new website. It will be an
incredible resource to both club members and prospective members.
The AKC English Springer Spaniel Health statement has been updated and approved by the
Board of Governors to align with our CHIC requirements. This will clarify the requirements for
the Breeder of Merit designation on the AKC site. The updated Health statement should be
posted on the AKC site shortly.

At the September 2024 BOG Meeting the ESSFTA Board of Governors approved the Linda MacKenzie Memorial
Trophy to commence in 2025.
The Linda MacKenzie Memorial Trophy
Annual National Specialty Award
Kellie FitzGerald and Bonnie Regan offer The Linda MacKenzie Memorial Trophy, to be awarded to the handler of
the Best of Breed Dog or Bitch at the ESSFTA National Specialty each year. The trophy is an antique sterling silver
stemmed bowl mounted on a wooden base. The donors will provide, each year, a large rosette to commemorate the
win, and the donors will also provide a plaque with the winner’s name, and the year of the win, which will be added
each year.
The trophy will remain in the possession of the ESSFTA; it must be won 10 times by the same person in order to be
permanently retired. Ownership of the trophy remains with the donors.
Linda MacKenzie, an active owner-exhibitor-breeder of English Springer Spaniels for many years, began showing
dogs at age 10 as a junior handler at matches. In her teens, she met Alice Berd (Pembroke Springers), who became
her lifelong mentor. Linda handled a number of Alice’s well-known English Springer Spaniels to their titles and be-
yond. She handled a number of Bonnie Regan’s winning Springers as well as those of others, and her handling was
not exclusive to English Springers. A dear friend and fondly remembered by so many, Linda passed away too soon
in April, 2014, of cancer.
This trophy honors and celebrates the skills and accomplishments of all who brilliantly present English Springer
Spaniels in conformation competition, winning the ultimate prize: Best of Breed at the National Specialty Show.
