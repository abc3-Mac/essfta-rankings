A Message From Dr Bev Sigl Felten
ESSFTA Bench Vice President & At Large Governor
January 2022 marks the start of a new year of dog events, with related challenges. Covid 19 is still very much a
massive element in all of our lives. The ESSFTA is hoping all of you can help enhance parent club events, as well
as strengthen activities that positively focus on the purpose bred, pure bred English Springer Spaniel. This ar-
ticle will highlight the new AKC Meet The Breed program.
Meet The Breeds
In 2021, the AKC started a new program designed to highlight the pure bred dog to audiences outside of formal
dog events. These programs were planned at separate venues, away from bench shows and other AKC events.
Unfortunately, due to Covid, the program was put on hold with a plan to begin again in 2022. Funding is availa-
ble for booth development and photos, see the AKC Meet The Breeds website for full details. The non- dog
event scheduled Meet The Breeds are purchased ticket entry events, but other events are being added includ-
ing those as part of regular dog events.
Non dog separate ticket entry AKC Meet the Breed events are scheduled at:
Fairplex Pomona, CA March 5-6
Tampa Convention Center, Tampa , FL April 23-24
Kay Bailey Hutchinson Convention Center, Dallas, TX June 25-26
San Diego Convention Center, San Diego, CA Sept 10-11
Atlanta Convention Center-Atlanta, GA Oct 22-23
Land O’Lakes Kennel Club -This writer was privileged to observe perhaps one of the first “Meet the Breed” ken-
nel club imbedded programs at the Land O’Lakes Show in St. Paul, Minnesota on January 8, 2022. This program
was upfront and center at a large all breed three-day cluster including the AKC Canine Emergency Disaster Re-
source Trailer, and the local K-9 law enforcement program. From the looks of the crowd, and the animated con-
versations going on with breed representatives, members of the public checking out the Canine Disaster Re-
source Trailer, and the beefy law enforcement officers gathered around that relaxed, uniformed, Dutch Shep-
herd K-9 officer, this event was a huge success.
Regional specialty clubs and members are encouraged to participate in both the non dog and club imbedded
Meet the Breed events. The National Pheasant Fest and Quail Classic has already made plans to participate in
Bird Dog Alley in Omaha Nebraska March 11-13. Please refer to the AKC Meet the Breeds webpage for specific
details on how to obtain resources for your local club to participate.
Safety Concerns
Theft
When most of us arrive at a dog event site, we usually are most concerned to get there, unload, and start doing
what we came to do. We get so focused on the prize, we sometimes forget very basic safety awareness. In cold
Minnesota early January, before the show, an exhibitor left her car running with her Boxer in it, to run into her
hotel. She returned to find the car, with the dog in it, gone. A large reward is now offered. We live in challeng-
ing times. Lock your car, watch out for people who look out of place, keep you and your dogs safe. This tragic
scenario could have happened anywhere.
Let’s work together to make 2022 a step forward.

Bloat
Submitted by Billie Kerfoot, Wynmoor ESS
Bloat occurs when gas forms in the stomach causing it to swell. As the stomach swells, you will notice the dog’s
sides swell out and become very tight. In less severe cases, dogs can eventually pass gas, have a bowel movement,
vomit, or burp to relieve the gas. However, in severe cases the stomach actually twists at both ends, which does
not allow the gas to pass. This is called torsion and is quite often fatal. Bloat is very painful, and some dogs will
lick continually to try to relieve the pain. They usually cannot lie down and will constantly walk around, whining.
It is terrible to watch.
Several years ago, we had a springer named Scarlett, who was only 18 months old the first time she bloated. After
three times, we had her stomach tacked. When Scarlett was about four or five, we learned that her father died of
bloat. Then Kitt, Scarlett’s grandfather on her mother’s side, bloated late one evening. Fortunately, we had him
on DogZymes which bought us enough time to save his life. In addition, a few years later, we bought a dog from
the West Coast and discovered that his grandmother died of bloat, his mother had bloated twice before dying of
bloat, his half-brother died of bloat, and his sister had bloated but not torsed, so she survived. We then heard of
other springers who had also bloated. Bloat, then, did not seem to be a problem of only big dogs – it was also
obviously a lesser known problem in springers.
There seems to be no real rhyme or reason for a dog to bloat. One of our friends fed their middle aged dog, went
out to dinner, and when they came back an hour or so later, he had bloated and died! Since Scarlett was Erin’s
dog, she decided to do a high school science fair project on how nutrition might affect bloat. She discovered that
foods with expandable grains took a lot longer to break down when water was added; however, adding probiotics
to the food tended to break down the kibble much faster. We did further research, and while we were never able
to pinpoint any one food, we did discover that bloat seems to happen when a dog does not have enough of the
correct enzymes to break down food in the stomach. Our research further led us to believe that several factors
seem to also be involved:
1- Familial – some families of dogs seem to have less of these good enzymes than others; although usually
deep chested breeds are more prone to bloat, any dog can bloat if it does not have the correct balance of en-
zymes
2- Stress seems to bring this on - in one case, the young male of a friend of ours died when the bitch in the kennel
next to him was in season. An older bitch died when a new baby was introduced into the house. Scarlett bloat-
ed but did not torse when Kitt died in his sleep at the house. You get the picture - it can be anything that causes
a dog to stress
3- Exercising right before or right after feeding heats up the body and can also lead to bloat
4- Foods based on barley, wheat, and other expandable grains can be a factor in causing the stomach to swell,
while rice and oat-based foods seem to have less of a chance of leading to bloat

Bloat (Cont.)
Submitted by Billie Kerfoot, Wynmoor ESS
Erin and I then did research on how to try to prevent bloat and what to do if a dog does bloat. This is what we
found:
1- If you have a dog who is a Hoover, inhaling food as if it is the last meal, you might consider getting a
slow feeder bowl which has little spikes that force the dog to eat more slowly. (The best bowl I found
was from Dog Supplies, called the Brake Fast Bowl)
2- If your dog has relatives who have bloated, be much more observant in his eating habits.
3- Feed your dog a product that is not based on corn or other expandable grains.
4- Add a probiotic supplement to help break down food in the stomach.
5- If your dog has a severe case of bloat one time, ask your vet about having its stomach tacked even if the
stomach did not twist.
6- Keep something like Gas –X on hand, and if you notice your dog beginning to swell a little after eating, give
him some which may help pass the gas caused by food swelling in the stomach.
7- Bloat can kill a dog very quickly, so if you notice your dog beginning to bloat, call your vet immediately!
Time is of the essence here!
8- And never become complacent! Many dogs who die from bloat only bloated that one time. Try to avoid
stressful situations, overeating, exercising, or eating too fast.
Signs of Bloat
1- Gagging or attempting to vomit, but nothing coming up, except ropey, slimy, marshmallow-like saliva
2- Extreme agitation
3- Pacing accompanied by the inability to sit or lay down comfortably
4- Swelling between the rib cage and the hips; it could start in the lower rib cage. (In early stages swelling
may not be visible. Insist your vet do an x-ray for early diagnosis. Once swelling is visible, time is limited.
This is an emergency!)
5- Heavy panting, often accompanied with labored and loud breathing
6- Head and tail hanging down, with a roached-up back
7- WATCH FOR UNUSUAL OR UNCHARACTERISTIC BEHAVIOR FOR YOUR DOG (e.g., a normally active dog
suddenly having no energy; a normally hungry dog refusing food; a laid-back dog who is suddenly
restless; or if your dog looks uncomfortable or in pain. Here is what the kennel manager said about
Peyton when he was in the kennel waiting to be shown at the national in 2014:
I'm glad to share and help in any way I can!! In Peyton's case, it definitely started out as one of those "gut feelings.” All was
normal in his routine that night from his activity level to his appetite. I was getting ready to leave the kennel and he normally
is sleeping on his pad but this night he was standing…and just looking at me different. I know it sounds strange…and subtle…
but that's how it started. At that time, I had no idea that bloat was such a problem, but I was familiar with it from seeing it in
other breeds. I got him out and felt his abdomen; it felt a little tense but not distended, and I felt he was standing a bit
hunched. I was concerned, so I took him home with me to keep an eye on him (really hoping it was just some back pain or
stiffness). I offered him water once we were home and he vomited very soon afterwards, which is definitely a bad sign….off
we went to the emergency center! It was touch and go, but thank God, they saved him! We're so lucky he started showing
symptoms when I was there. Go with your gut, it's always better to be safe than sorry!
Bottom line, be observant! You do not have to be paranoid, but if you notice any of the symptoms that might
indicate bloat, call your vet and get your dog to the clinic ASAP! Better safe, than sorry!

SCENT WORK & YOUR ESS
JEAN HILBIG
Have you and your Springer tried one of the Scent
Detection Sports yet? All signs are pointing to
Scent Work being a popular sport with our
members and their Springers. The AKC Springer
Only Scent Work Trial held in conjunction with
the ESSFTA National in Dallas in 2021 was a
raving success. We had 128 entries by 39 dogs.
The trial was held at the Audubon Swim Park and
Recreation Center in Garland Texas. What an
awesome site for a scent work trial. Dogs
searched the concession stands, the wading pool,
the shower houses, the storage rooms, and the
kitchen. And we were done in time for everybody
to make it back for the Welcome Party!
Plans for the 2022 National Springer only Scent
Work Trial are close to final. We will be back at
Purina Farms. The Scent Work trial will begin
Monday afternoon with all levels of Interior and
Detective searches in Gateway Hall. Containers
will be held in Gateway Hall early Tuesday
morning so we can vacate and let exhibitors move
into the Grooming Area. Exteriors and Buried
Searches will be on the Main Show field on
Tuesday. Our judges will be Peter Betchley and
Kathy Echols. If you participated in Dallas in the
Pool Palooza Search Party on Friday afternoon,
you will remember that Peter was the Judge for
that fun event.
It is not too late to start training your dog to
participate in the 2022 National Scent Work
Trial. Find a trainer in our area and start sniffin’.
Hope to see you in 2022.
