Breeders Education
Pedigree Research: How OFA can help
Orthopedic Foundation for Animals is more than just a repository for health clearances. It can
be an instrumental tool in researching pedigrees of your existing dogs, or for planning litters.
Especially for newer breeders who may not have known dogs in their pedigrees personally,
this is a great way to get to know the family behind your dogs. A few things to remember:
Recommended clearances have changed over time. If you’re looking up dogs born
before the year 2000, you may see mostly hip and eye clearances.
Some breeders may have tested their dogs without submitting to OFA. If you h ave a
question about a dogs clearances, it’s always best to contact the breeder or owner directly.
Even dogs with all their clearances can produce offspring with health issues.
Preservation breeders are conscientious—no one sets out to intentionally cre ate a
problem.
First, go to the website OFA.org.

ber or a kennel name, and select the dog you are looking for. The screen will look like this:

ESSFTA Youth Event Scholarship
Submitted by Cathy Flanders, Corresponding Secretary
Purpose:
The ESSFTA Youth Events Scholarship is designed to support young persons who have participated in
AKC sponsored events with an English Springer Spaniel. The scholarship honors applicants who have ac-
tively participated in English Springer Spaniel related competitions in the field, show and p erformance
rings. This scholarship emphasizes the importance of dedication allowing applicants to sh owcase their
unique contributions to the English Springer Spaniel breed and their future aspirations. It celebrates those
who are passionate about the breed and who have positively impacted the English Springer Spaniel com-
munity.
Award Amount:
The applicant can submit reimbursement up to $150 in a calendar year (January 1-December 31) .
Eligibility Criteria:
1. Age: Applicants must be between 9-18 years old.
2. Membership: The applicant must be a member of ESSFTA at the time of participating in the event.
To become a member, join on the ESSFTA website (englishspringerspaniels.org). Junior membership is
$10 per calendar year.
3. Dog: The dog must be an English Springer Spaniel registered with the AKC and at least 6 months of age.
4. Reimbursement: Applicants must provide evidence, in a manner which can be confirmed with AKC records,
of their participation with an English Springer Spaniel at an AKC sanctioned event. Applicant must provide a copy
of entry fees with this application. Fees will be reimbursed to the applicant’s parent upon approval by the Youth
Events Committee. The applicant can submit reimbursement up to $150 in a calendar year (January 1-December
31). Applications must be submitted by January 15 of the following year to:
Cathy Flanders, ESSFTA Corresponding Secretary
secretary@englishspringerspaniels.org

ESSFTA Delegate Report
Submitted by Cristy Joy
A Message from Your New AKC Delegate: Cristy Joy on Representing
ESSFTA at the National Level
To the ESSFTA Community,
I’m honored to step into the role of AKC Delegate for the English Springer Spanie l Field Trial
Association. As many of you know, our breed is not just a passion—it’s a way of life. As your
new representative within the American Kennel Club’s Delegates, I’m here to carry that spirit
forward—and make sure our voice is heard loud and clear.
What Is an AKC Delegate?
Let’s start with what this role actually means. As a Parent Club, ESSFTA holds a seat with the
AKC’s Delegates, which serves as a governing body that influences key policies and elects the
AKC Board. Delegates are also conduits between AKC leadership and breed cl ubs—helping
shape policies and offering feedback from the grassroots up, shaping the future of dog sports,
breeding regulations, and the standing of our breeds.
Why This Matters Now!
The AKC landscape is changing rapidly—technology, policy shifts, public pressur e, and evolv-
ing breeding standards are impacting us all. Here are a few pressing issues I’ll be tracking
closely and where I plan to advocate for ESSFTA’s interests:
Preservation Breeding and Legislative Threats: States and localities are introducing
legislation that could hinder responsible breeding. We must stay alert and vocal in
defending our right to breed with integrity and purpose.
Event Accessibility and Declining Participation: Field trials, conformation shows, and
performance events are seeing shifting demographics. It’s time to think about how to
sustain our sports, attract new participants, and reduce barriers to entry—especially for
young handlers, and newcomers.
Judging and Breed Standards: The Parent Club must continue to protect and educate
around our breed standard and be involved in how judges are trained, evaluated, and
promoted.
Modern Club Culture: We’re being called to ensure that our clubs are welcoming,
inclusive, and reflective of the passion that brings all types of people to the breed. This
includes mentoring programs, clearer pathways for involvement, and transparency.

Stay Safe Online: A Message from Your Digital Technology Officer
Dear ESSFTA Members,
As your Digital Technology Officer, I want to take a moment to remind everyone—especially our longtime and valued
members—to be vigilant when navigating online spaces, including email and social media. Unfortunately, scam activity targeting
clubs like ours is on the rise.
Here are a few important reminders to help keep you safe:
Email Scams Are Getting Smarter
Be cautious of emails that look like they're from ESSFTA or one of our officers but seem "off." If an email asks you to:
• Purchase gift cards or pay an invoice
•
• Click on a suspicious link
•
• Urgently wire money
•
• Or sounds unusually demanding or out of character
•
Stop and verify. Reach out directly to the person by phone or through a known, trusted email—not by replying to the
suspicious message.

Fake Apparel Posts on Facebook
We’ve also seen an increase in Facebook posts advertising ESSFTA or "National" gear that we did not authorize. These often
pop up in the comments section or are shared in groups. While they may use our logo or name, these sellers are not affiliated

When in doubt, don’t click. Always buy club gear directly through official ESSFTA channels.
How You Can Protect Yourself
• Don’t click on links from people you don’t know
•
• Never share your passwords or banking information online
•
• Be suspicious of “too good to be true” deals or emotional pleas for money
•
• Ask a family member or fellow club member if something doesn’t feel right
Our strength as a community lies in looking out for one another— both in the field and online.

Honesty, Kindness, and Encouragement:
What is sportsmanship?
Often a day competing with our dogs reminds us how happy it makes us to see beautiful dogs—ours,
that those of others—and to feel the pride, care and devotion humans devote to their canine companions.
Good feelings, warm conversations and gentle advice we encounter at a dog show build deep feelings of
satisfaction for the day we spent at the show. Our positive impressions are often based o n spending time
with other exhibitors who understand that good sportsmanship is the lifeblood of our sport. Sharing kind-
ness, encouragement and generous advice bring in new participants and ensures experi enced folks keep
coming back. Mother Teresa wisely said, "Kind words can be short and easy to speak, but their echoes
are truly endless."
The AKC Code of Sportsmanship states, “The sport of purebred dog competitive events dates prior to
1884, the year of AKC’s birth. Shared values of those involved in the sport include principles of sports-
manship. They are practiced in all sectors of our sport: conformation, performance and companion. Many
believe that these principles of sportsmanship are the prime reason why our sport has thrived or over one
hundred years.”

Submitted by Sarah Ferrell
Thank you to the many people who shared their examples of good sportsmanship that have lingered in
their memories. In the following memories shared, I have chosen to omit identification of witnesses and
dogs.
Great Sportsmanship Is Brave, Generous and Honest
“Being a good sport means you know you have a good dog, you know you did well, you know you are
another exhibitor’s competition; but they beat you-- and it brings you genuine joy to watch another com-
petitor when they realize they won …. When you compete a lot and years later, you reflect upon fond
memories--these are the memories you remember.”
“At a Junior Showmanship competition, a Senior handler set aside a great deal of time during a very
tight schedule to assist two Junior handlers. This was at the expense of preparing her own dog to the ex-
tent she would have desired, but greatly benefited the two Junior handlers. The Senior ha ndler barely
made it to her own ring on time, but showed her own dog well. Immediately, when finished in her Senior
ring, she went to the two Junior handlers and sat ringside with them during the Intermediate handling lev-
el, to answer questions and point out specific handling techniques. This was a wonderful example of
helping promote new handlers into the sport.”
“A field handler was running at the Master level. The cover where the dog flushed a bird was very thick
and tall, with numerous shrubs within the area. Due to the cover and terrain, it was extremely difficult for
the judges to see all the details of the dog’s behavior. The dog had handled wonderfully throughout the
entire course. On this particular flush, the dog was unable to maintain it’s position after th e flush. While
it did not chase after the bird, it definitely moved a significant distance after being commanded to “hup”
after the flush. The judges were unable to see if the dog had moved or stayed, but it was obvious to the
handler. When the dog returned with the retrieve, the handler graciously handed the bird to the judges
and thanked them for their time. When the judges looked confused, the handler explained to the judges
that the dog had not held his “hup” and thus had to receive a non-qualifying score. It would have been
very easy for that handler to simply walk away and never point out his dog’s mistake. This was a fantastic
example of understanding the true goal of the testing program and taking pride in doing the right thing for
the sport.”
