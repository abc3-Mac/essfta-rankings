The Presidents Message – Reflecting on Three Years of Leadership
As my tenure as President of the English Springer Spaniel Field Trial Association (ESSFTA) comes to a close, I
am proud of the progress we've made together. These achievements are a testament to the dedication and hard
work of our Officers, Board of Governors, committees, and members who have contributed their time and ex-
pertise. Over the past three years, we've engaged our members, updated the By-Laws to use tec hnology,
strengthened governance as we created a process for transparency, improved financial stability, and fostered
youth involvement—laying a solid foundation for the future of our breed and sport.
2025 Milestones:
• Approval of the Committees-Best Practice document, which was designed to refine commi ttee operations,
ensuring they function efficiently and align with our organization’s mission.
• Readdressed the ESSFTA Lifetime Achievement Award at the Board of Governor level to be modified and
rolled out later in 2025.
• The Breed Illustrated Standard is on the final step for approval.
2025 Initiatives:
• Developed a monthly bulletin called "Spring into Action" to keep our members informed on current work
and progress.
• Established the Financial Committee recognizing the importance of fiscal responsibility Current members
include Steve Hoostal, Ted Lagala, (Chair), Elizabeth Purkaple, Kathy Nestor and Lori Nieto.
• Established the Youth Events Committee. We took proactive steps to support youth engagement by form-
ing the Youth Events Committee. Under the leadership of Co-Chairs Kim Bullard and Josh Riddle,
alongside members Cathy Flanders, Mike Nolan, and Kathy Patregnani and Jill Hibbard will help foster
young talent and enhance participation in our events. Their work will ensure the next generation remains
actively involved and passionate about our mission.
• Developed an application and process for members to volunteer on Judges Education subcommittees and
will welcome 10 new members. Committees include: Educational Seminar and Hands-on Workshop,
Ringside Mentoring and Tutoring, Breed Illustrated Standard and Educational Materials. Virtual Breed
Course Development and AKC’s Canine College.
• Honored an outstanding member with a Lifetime Recognition Membership.
Plan for the 100th Anniversary of the ESSFTA Parent Club in 2026.
• Partnered with two organizations to help support victims impacted by recent natural disasters by donating

ESSFTA Bench Governors Report
Submitted by Liz Pike
Judges Ed and Illustrated Standard
Erin Kerfoot, Judges Education Committee Chair, and her team have been hard at work refining the educational
materials and providing presentations at both Spaniel Club and at the Advanced Judges Institute at the Kentucky
cluster. Erin’s detailed report will follow - we are grateful to Erin and the team for continuing to provide high
quality education to AKC judges
We are also pleased to report that the Illustrated Standard Task Force has made significant progress towards fi-
nalizing the new Illustrated Standard. Several key illustrations have been completed, and we appr eciate the input
we received from members that helped further refine this important document.
The team has received excellent applications for all four of the judges’ education committees – Educational Semi-
nar and Hands-on Workshop, Ringside Mentoring and Tutoring, Breed Illustrated Standard and Educational Mate-
rials, and Virtual Breed Course Development and AKC’s Canine College. The final recommendat ions for commit-
tee membership will be presented for approval at the Board of Governors meeting at the Annual Parent Club
Meeting in April.
National Specialty Update
All of us are sadly aware of the devastation that resulted from a tornado at Purina Farms. The staff at Purina have
been in touch with clubs scheduled to hold shows and the most recent update from them is the following:
On March 14, Purina Farms was directly hit by a tornado, which caused extensive damage to our property. Our Visitor
Center, Barn, Pet Center and the Incredible Dog Arena were the hardest hit. Thankfully, our staff and all 160+ animals in
our care remained safe and accounted for. We are currently working diligently to assess the full extent of property
damage.
In addition to the primary facilities mentioned, we are also assessing repairs needed for the surrounding areas, including the
RV park hookups, which will impact our ability to welcome Clubs back to the facility. Fortunately, our Event Center did not
sustain a direct hit like other areas, and we believe with some minor repairs, it will be fully operational soon.
As always, safety remains our top priority. We will continue to share updates with you as new information becomes
available.
As we receive additional updates, we will send them out to our members.
The National Specialty committee is finalizing information for the website and will be posting reservations and
event information soon. We have exciting news for those who are looking for more opportunities – we are
planning to offer TWO regional Specialties on Tuesday, August 19th, one in the morning and one in the afternoon
and a single sweepstakes event on Monday evening, August 18th. Entries for the regular shows will be limited to
100 dogs for each show. Puppy sweepstakes will be limited to 50 entries and there will be no limit on Veteran
Sweepstakes. We have just finalized judging contracts for the Tuesday shows and can share that we will have Ms.
Paula Nykiel for the morning show and Ms. Linda Robey for the afternoon show. Check back over the next few
weeks for additional information on all the National Specialty events.

ESSFTA Bench Governors Report
Submitted by Liz Pike
Last year, when AKC increased the upper age limit for Sweepstakes to 24 months, we sent out a survey to
members asking if they wanted to keep the upper age limit at the National Specialty at 18 months or increase it
to 24 months. The results showed that more members voted to keep the upper age limit at 18 months, albeit by
a small margin. We decided to survey the members again this year and added an option for Junior Puppy Sweeps
(6 months to 15 months) and Senior Puppy Sweeps (15 months to 24 months). These will be separate Sweep-
stakes events with each group receiving a Best in Specialty Sweepstakes and the Best of Opposi te Specialty
Sweepstakes. The poll will close at noon EDT on April 1 – we want to hear from you!
General Updates
We have been asked to consider petitioning AKC to make English Springer Spaniels a ramp mandatory breed.
Many exhibitors feel that using a ramp gives the judge a better perspective on the overall appearance of the dogs
as well as preventing the judge from coming up over the dog. Some mentioned that it is also ea sier on the
handler!
You will be receiving a poll asking if you want us to petition AKC to make ESS a ramp mandatory breed; if that is
the majority opinion, we will need to go to AKC for their concurrence.
Annual Parent Club Meeting
I hope you will join us on Friday, April 25th and Saturday, April 26th for the Annual Parent Club meetings. On
Friday, the Bench Governors will have a work session to discuss progress against our strategic plan, new
initiatives, and emerging issues. On Saturday we will have both the Membership meeting and the Board of
Governors meeting – we invite you to join us and provide your input!
We, as a governor group, are committed to the principles of inclusion, communication, and transparency. We
are reaching out to members with surveys to better understand your opinions when we have decisions to make.
Both your regional and at large governors are available and want to hear from you!

ESSFTA Field VP Report
Submitted by Mike Nolan
This will be my final entry as I will be replaced at the Parent Club Meeting in Oma ha next
month. It was my pleasure to act as the Field VP for 5 years but it is time to pass the torch. I
appreciated everyone who has contributed to this game and has moved it forward through over
100 years of Field Trials. This does not happen without a great deal of help and commitment
from all of you.
I would like to close with one item that has become a bit more pronounced since the adoption of
Hunt Secretary. People are waiting until the very last minute to determine what tr ials they are
going to participate in in the upcoming week. They do this for obvious reasons but it has
adverse consequences on your local trials. Birds must be ordered and not knowin g how many
to acquire at current prices can be expensive. We need to try and do a better job at this issue.
Putting on a trial is a great deal of work. Let’s all help out in this area whenever possible.
I want to leave with a couple of comments and requests:
• Please be mindful of criticism of judges/guns…especially in the Trial….No On e sets out to
make your life miserable…..we are all human and we make mistakes.
• Treat your fellow handlers with good sportsmanship
• Help in anyway you can….shag…..give another a ride….pick up trash…..we a re in this
together
It IS ALL ABOUT THE DOGS…….

ESSFTA Judges Education
Submitted by Erin Kerfoot
judgeseducation@englishspringerspaniels.org
The Judges Education Committee (JEC) has been hard at work, and we are excited to sha re updates on
our ongoing and upcoming projects.
Current/Ongoing Projects
Ringside Mentoring Updates
We have reached out to previous ringside mentors to confirm their continued participation as breed
mentors. If you were previously a ringside mentor and have not been contacted, please reach out to Erin.
An updated list of approved mentors will soon be available on both the ESSFTA and AKC websites. To
ensure consistency and accessibility, we will also be sending updated learning materials to mentors to
share with mentees.
Breed Seminars & Workshops
The JEC has conducted well-received breed seminars at American Spaniel Club in Knoxville, TN, and
the American Dog Show Judges Institute in Louisville, KY. Feedback has been overwhelmingly positive,
with upcoming judges eagerly anticipating the completion of our Breed Illustrated Standa rd (IS).
Breed Illustrated Standard (IS) Development
Thanks to member feedback, the subcommittee has outlined edits and modifications to the IS. Ruth
Dehmel and Sarah Willms have joined as consultants to provide additional expert guidance. We are
excited to share updates on two head illustrations—a side profile and a straight-on view o f the male head.
Your input has been invaluable, and we appreciate the continued support!
JEC Subcommittee Appointments
To better serve our mission, we have organized the JEC into four specialized subcommittees:
Educational Seminar and Hands-on Workshop
Breed Illustrated Standard and Educational Materials
Ringside Mentoring and Tutoring
Virtual Breed Course Development and AKC's Canine College
Applications for subcommittee membership were reviewed and recommendations for subcommittee
placements were made to the Board of Governors (BOG). The BOG will make final decisions and formal
announcements of committee membership will be made at the ESSFTA annual meeting.
Upcoming Projects
Educational Seminar & Hands-on Workshop
Finalizing our seminar presentation with illustrations from the IS, field trial videos, and interactive
elements.
Exploring the possibility of an annual webinar to expand reach to judges nationwide.
Hosting our next seminar and hands-on workshop at the Advanced Judging Institute in Houston, TX
(July 2025).

Committee Best Practices
English Springer Spaniel Field Trial Association
Changes from the original document are in red below.
Standing committees and task forces are the lifeblood of the organization and the volunteers in those groups
are vital to advancing the objectives of the Parent Club.
The President, Executive Committee or the Board of Governors may each year appoint standing commit-
tees to advance the work of the Club in such matters as dog shows, performance events, field trials,
trophies, annual prizes, membership and other initiatives which may well be served by comm ittees. Special
committees/ task forces may also be appointed to aid it in particular projects. All committees are always
subject to the final authority of the Board of Governors.
The Board of Governors provides oversight for committees/task forces and as such, provid es the following
Best Practices for ESSFTA Committees and Task Forces:
1. Committee chairpersons are responsible for defining the mission of the committee a nd
responsibilities, authority, and expectations for committee members; this information must be sent
to the Corresponding Secretary for publication on the ESSFTA website.
2. Lists of committee chairpersons and committee members must be updated annually prior to the
annual Parent Club meeting; chairpersons are responsible for providing that information to the
Corresponding Secretary. This updated list will be reviewed by the BOG at the Parent Club
Annual Meeting.
3. At such time as a committees and/or Task Forces has concluded their work and are no longer
active, the Chairperson must report this to the Executive Committee and the Corresponding
Secretary for updating on the ESSFTA website.
4. Chairpersons should strive to recruit committee members with relevant expertise and diverse
opinions. All chairs and committee members must be ESSFTA members in good standing.
5. Rotation of committee members should take into account a balance between providing opportunity
and fresh thinking with the need for continuity and historical perspective.
6. Each committee will establish term limits and rotation schedules for both the chair and members of
the committee. The Board of Governors will review and approve the term limits and rotation
schedules. The Board of Governors are authorized to replace committee members or chairpersons
who are inactive or fail to abide by the stated Best Practices.

spring of hope… we had everything before us,” Charles Dickens could have had the joys and fears of raising the best
possible puppy in mind when he penned those opening thoughts in “A Tale of Two Cities”.
As dog breeders and puppy raisers, we should add, ‘It was the best advice; it was the worst ad vice.’ How many
little pups have been tortured when owners follow bad advice given with good intentions? To insure that our pup
receives every opportunity for optimal mental and physical development, we often need to stop, take a calming
breath and remember that a good experience from the pup’s viewpoint depends on the pup’s very individual tem-
perament, prior experience or lack of experience, and on the genes that made the pup who he is.
Baby steps are best for baby dogs. ‘Socialize, socialize, socialize!’ can become good advice gone bad. Enroll in
a puppy kindergarten class where all the dogs are very young, the class is small, and the breeds are evenly matched
in size and play drive. Do the class instructors have keen eyes for signs of pup intimidation, over-exuberance or
frightened pup responses? Build a foundation for your pup to love to leave home and to delight inother dogs. Not
all classes are managed well for puppy behaviors, nor are all pups screened carefully enough to set the stage for
happy learning experiences. Before you take your pup to a class, ask if you may come and watch a class. Most in-
structors will be happy to have you. Leave your pup at home until you have seen the size of the class and the size
of the pups in the class, surfaces, noise levels, and type of instruction. Watch class interactions closely and note the
stress levels of dogs and owners.

Learning to read your pup’s reactions to the expanding world around him is a constant focus of bonding with
each new dog. Each pup is different. If a pup goes toward a situation with a wagging tail and soft e ye expression,
he probably feels the new experience is safe. If a pup plants his little feet and refuses to take a step toward any
new person or object, respect his reluctance. Let the pup take a step back. Let the pup watch from the sidelines
while he works to understand his new world. Pushing a pup into an experience that frightens him may have the
opposite effect than teaching him to be outgoing and accepting of new situations. Keep play sessi ons in new places
brief. Always leave a new experience with the pup happy to have been there.
Your puppy is not a tool for older dogs to use to reinforce their experience that the world is safe. Have you
witnessed an adolescent dog, on leash, dragging their owner full-speed-ahead toward a tiny pup? Usually, this out
of control owner is shouting, “ He’s okay! He loves puppies!” This is the time to happily pat your leg, sweetly say
your pup’s name and scamper to another spot. You can be friendly, and say, ‘My pup needs a little space!’ or a
similar message that you hope will make the clueless dog owner reel in his untrained dog. The one thing you must
not do is offer your inexperienced pup up to be overwhelmed by a dog whose behavior, temperament and play
techniques are unknown to you.

Puppyhood is not the time to make a bucket list of every life experience you want your pup to enjoy and force
him into myriad confusing, perhaps noisy, often frightening situations. Have you ever seen a little pup quivering,
with eyes rolled back to show the whites at a loud gathering like a music concert, or a fireworks event? Thrusting
a young dog into an over-stimulating atmosphere will harm the pup, not build the pup’s confidence that the world
outside his house is safe. Many stores allow pups to shop with their owners. Rather than taking your pup to a pet
store where many dogs may go, and where you have no way of knowing the health or vaccination history of those
dogs,take your pup to places where few dogs go, but where the pup is allowed to sit in your shopping cart and see
the world roll by. National chain hardware stores, many craft stores, garden centers, restaurants with outdoor
seating and many businesses welcome well-behaved pups.if your pup is small enough to tuck inside your jacket and
both you and your pup are quiet and polite, chances are high no one will object to your pup’s pr esence.
When you look into your pup’s trusting eyes, realize that to the pup you are the entire world. Tell yourself, “Easy
does it.” Go slowly, with joy for your pup and an eagle eye on the world around him. Show him a world that is
safe and calm.
