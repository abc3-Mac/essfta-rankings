The Executive Committee Teamwork for the ESSFTA
By Cristy Joy, ESSFTA President
The 2023 Executive Committee has been meeting monthly on the third Tuesday of the month to
track the implementation the ESSFTA goals, address concerns raised by the Parent Club Membership
and keep committees on track. The group has had 2 meetings, working on issues facing the Parent
Club. The Committee is comprised of Cristy Joy (President), Judy Manley (Bench/Performance VP)
and Mike Nolan (Field VP). Cathy Flanders (Corresponding Secretary) participates as the Recorder, a
non-voting member.
The Executive Committee Teamwork items implemented include the completion of the AKC Preserva-
tion Bank Survey; Judges Survey Completed for the AKC National Championship (December in Orlan-
do); Breed Revision Results sent to the AKC for their review, discussion and implementation; the
President's May Flowers – Take 2 – June Jubilee went to the Membership; the Judges Education
Preparations are in full swing including the video prep of the Field Trial Judging webinar is July 19th at
Pineshadows in Brainerd, Minnesota.
The group’s strategy is address issues through Teamwork. They will gather information, involve the
necessary people, analyze items and formulate a strategy to help the good of the cause. The items
currently on the agenda include but are not limited to the following:
Goal with Progress to submit the revised Bylaws to the AKC. Verify the review is completed by
BOG and Membership and comments are vetted then addressed
Goal with Progress working with the Web Site Committee (WSC) on the Web Site RFP process
implementation.
Goal with Progress to complete Strategic Planning and implement next steps
Goal with Progress to improve the judging of the AKC ESS competition styles.
Assisting in the process for the AKC Conformance Judge teaching in December at Orlando
Working with the Leaders of the AKC ESS Field Trial Apprentice Program, Judge Teaching
Programs and updating the FT Rules and Blue Book.
Creating an environment for implementation and creating teaching techniques and videos
for all ESS competition types. (Conformation and Performance which includes Field Tri-
als)
Goal with Progress to track and assist Committees for the implementation of items approved by
the ESSFTA Board of Governors.
Goal with Progress providing guidance with the Breed Standard Committee. A strong focus to
bring the Breed Illustration Standard discussion to completion.
Current President’s work includes working on the appointments, informing the Membership as
much as possible, address Member issues and work with the English Cocker Spaniel Parent
Club on Field Trial concerns and day-to-day conversations.
If you have items that need the ESSFTA Board of Governor’s attention please contact the
Executive Committee members.

By Cristy Joy, ESSFTA President
The ESSFTA is in need of volunteers to fill the following positions:
Membership Secretary
The Membership Secretary is an officer of the ESSFTA and elected annually by the Board of
Governors. Duties include, but are not limited to:
• Managing all aspects of membership renewals
• Processing new membership applications
• Serving as a liaison with the treasurer on financial aspects of membership
• Working with the Corresponding Secretary on club records and reports
• Communicating with the membership about privileges of membership and forwards
questions and concerns to appropriate sources.
• Setting up the new membership program on the refreshed website
• Attending, in person, the Annual Meeting and other ESSFTA meetings whenever possible.
Interviews for the Membership Secretary position start June 30th and run
through July 9th.
If you are interested in this position, please contact Cristy Joy, President
(cjoy@archi-etc.com or 402-429-7150) to set up an interview.
Legislative Liaison
The Legislative Liaison is an ad hoc position that helps the AKC, ESSFTA and individual pure-
bred fanciers work together to address legislative issues efficiently and effectively. Legislative
Liaisons follow local and state-level policy issues (typically through AKC postings) and provide
a communication link between the ESSFTA and the AKC Government Relations depart-
ment. Responsibilities include, but are not limited to:
• Receiving materials, updates and advice from the AKC Government Relations Department
and distributing information to club members.
• Organizing and motivating members to become active in the legislative process and encour-
aging other interested fanciers to help advance legislation that will benefit purebred dog owners
and breeders.
• Working with the Board of Governors and members should a legislative initiative arise that
requires input from purebred dog owners and breeders.
Alerting the AKC to pending legislative issues in their communities or states.
If you are interested in this position, please contact Cristy Joy, President
(cjoy@archi-etc.com or 402-429-7150)

ENGLISH SPRINGER SPANIEL FOUNDATION
Francie Nelson
Thinking about an estate gift, or a gift of memorabilia, to the Springer Foundation?
The English Springer Foundation encourages, and highly values, estate gifts and gifts of memorabilia
such as books, historic photographs, artwork, or collectibles. The Archives Committee, a joint project of
the Foundation and the ESSFTA, has received some wonderful bequests of historic and highly collectible
items that have resulted from the careful planning and generosity of members of our Springer community.
The Foundation has also received direct financial bequests that have resulted from thoughtful donors who
have included us in their estate planning. Keep in mind that – different from ESSFTA – the Foundation is a
charity, and gifts are tax deductible to the limits described by law.
If you’re thinking about a bequest to the Springer Foundation, or if you might be thinking about an ultimate
“home” for valuable collectibles – artwork, books, or memorabilia from your years with English Springer
Spaniels, you might find this information helpful and useful.
You’ll want the help of your attorney or tax preparer to refine your intentions and to help you understand
the tax implications of estate planning. Once you have decided on a plan of action, here’s what will help
the English Springer Foundation to record and recognize your generosity:
1. If you include the Foundation in a will, a copy of the section that provides the nature of the bequest and
the amount, as well as a copy of the notarized signature(s) and date signed, is most helpful. A simple
letter informing us of your intent is fine, too, especially if you include the name and address of your
executor/legal representative.
We ensure the confidentiality of the documents that we receive. If your intentions change over time, all
you need to do is let us know. Most bequest and estate gifts are not permanent and we understand that
plans change.
2. If this is an intended gift or bequest of a current asset (life insurance, real estate, stock, annuities/
retirement funds, etc.) it’s most helpful to know the type of asset and its current value. Again, a letter of
intent that describes the asset is sufficient; include contact information for your executor/legal
representative if possible.
3. If the intended bequest (or immediate gift) is tangible property (jewelry, artwork, books, etc.) we need
an independent appraisal provided by the donor. If you are not concerned about immediate tax
consequences, then a simple list, and a letter of intent, is fine; however, there is no tax benefit until the
intended property is actually received.
The Archives Committee has a process for the intake of memorabilia; if you want to know more, I am
happy to connect you with the Archives Committee. Just send me an email:
fanfarespringers@comcast.net
I also have a simple form available if you would simply like to alert us of an intended future
bequest; send me an email: fanfarespringers@comcast.net

60th Anniversary of the ESSFTA National Amateur
Championship—Central Nebraska
Submitted by Cristy Joy
It is a great honor to be hosting the 60th Anniversary of the English Springer Spaniel Field Trial
Association National Amateur Championship in Central Nebraska. This year’s judges, Todd Stelzer and
Jerry Barrett will have the honor of selecting the “best of the best”. The event’s headqua rters is at the
River’s Edge Convention Center in Columbus Nebraska. The series work will be north of Silver Creek
and southeast of Fullerton Nebraska. Coming together to create such a wonderful event requires a
strong team. We are thrilled to have the three Nebraska Clubs, the National Guard, school groups such
as Future Farmers of America, Economic Development groups, and the Rocky Mountain Interclub
volunteering to work together for such a tremendous occasion. A big thank you to the Nebraska
Tourism Commission for providing the event with a Community Impact grant.
The scope of the National Amateur is impressive, as it is one of the few remaining competitions that
creates true hunting conditions over variable terrain and through all types of weather. Field trailers with
their English Springer Spaniels qualify throughout the year by placing in local trials. The O wners/handlers
can enter the Championship trial for $325 per dog. The Championship Field Trial will focus on the
English Springer Spaniels, judging their ability to find, flush and retrieve shot game during five land series
and a water series over several days of competition. The Judges will invite the dog and handler to
continue to participate in the progressing series work. At the conclusion of the first series, each judge
selects the dogs from among those who have performed under them that they consider worthy of con-
tinuing in the stake and they are put down in sequence under the other judge. These procedures are fol-
lowed through successive series until the judges are satisfied that they are prepared to make the awards.
The Field Trial Team will keep the focus on the dogs and handlers as they stake the courses in amazing,
safe upland grass terrain and plant great flying pheasants to elevate the competition.
The competition will be fierce as we hunt the beautiful upland game landscape. It’s extraordinary to wit-
ness, and it helps to build an exciting social environment as well. This trial is not only a competition, but
it is also a wonderful gathering of like-minded people. Each Club member brings their own exciting
element, whether it be a raffle, a silent auction, or a big cook off. For this year’s trial, there will be a
lunch and dinner provided for all of the competition days accept the last evening where dinner will be on

National Amateur Championship—Central Nebraska
Jerry Barrett — Judge Bio
Submitted by Cristy Joy
2023 National Amateur Championship
Jerry Barrett
Judge
My involvement in Springer Spaniel field trials began 25 years ago when I set out on a search for a hunting dog to
replace my Labrador that had recently passed. One of my close friends told me that I should consider a Springer
Spaniel as they were great companions, excellent upland dogs and would also fit the bill for my limited waterfowl
interests too.
During the process of searching for a “started” Springer I was also able to see some well-trained field trial
Springers. My immediate response was “I want one of those”. A short while later I had my first field bred Springer
and it was just the beginning of a long and rewarding relationship with this great sport.
In the last 25 years I have I have participated in Springer Spaniel field trials all across the US and Canada. That
experience has been very rewarding in experiences and also has resulted in many friendships.
I have chaired numerous field trials including 2 National Championships, and I have shot many all-age trials in both
countries. My judging experience includes over 35 all-age trials in the US and Canada and a US National Open
Championship.
It’s truly an honor to judge your dogs in the 2023 National Amateur Championship. I don’t take that responsibility
lightly, as I fully understand the amount of time, effort and significant personal investment that an amateur handler
commits to get each dog qualified and compete in this event.
I wish the best of luck and the opportunity to excel in performance to each of the dogs and their handlers.

National Amateur Championship—Central Nebraska
Judge Bio’s
Submitted by Cristy Joy
2023 National Amateur Championship
Todd Stelzer
Judge
I was born and raised in Cadott, WI. A small little town in Central
Wisconsin, where nothing is happening all the time. As a teenager,
when I went to get the cows in the fall I would take my Grandfathers
springer with me and every now and again I’d shoot a couple of
roosters. That dog was a renegade, but he could find birds. Today
there are very few pheasants in our area.
Thankfully, both of my Grandfathers loved to hunt and taught me the
trade. Neither was worth a spit as a dog trainer but they loved hunting.
I spent many afternoons in the woods with them hunting grouse and
loved every second of it.
I got my first springer spaniel from a back yard breeder. She wasn’t the
most athletic dog but she would find birds. After doing some research, I
went to my first springer field trial in Minnesota. When I got home I
told my wife that our dog might be the same breed as the field trial
springer, but not the same dog.
I started competition in the run and gun series with a few dogs. Even though it was fun, I didn’t feel it was a good
evaluation of the dog. I eventually started competing in the AKC field trails. My first field trial dog was the
National High Point Puppy which hooked me totally to the sport. Her name was Brandy and she was probably the
most talented dog I’ve had my hands on and the foundation bitch of my kennel. From that point on the springer
spaniels and the field trials just exploded for me. I never had any intention of becoming a professional dog trainer.
Now 25 years later I have a kennel full of client dogs that trust me
enough to train and compete with their dogs across the nation. I can’t thank them enough. I also am Thankful to
all the people that supported me, guided me, offered their homes to me, and mentored me through the years.
Most of all I need to thank my family, especially my lovey wife Ann. Being a professional dog trainer isn’t very
glamorous, especially at midnight in a rain storm waiting for dogs to do their business. But Ann has endured it all,
me being gone months at a time, not getting projects done and dog crap everywhere. She’s a trooper.
Finally, thank you to all, it is a huge honor to be selected as the 2023 ESS National Amateur judge. I am
extremely passionate about springer spaniels and AKC Field trials. I am very excited for the opportunity to judge
the best springer spaniels in the Nation.

SARAH A. FERRELL
Tracking Down Education, Information, Shopping and Fun on the World Wide Web
Don’t Rush In:
Take a moment to visualize the winning outcome
Are professional handlers more talented than owner-handlers? One huge difference between non-professional
conformation exhibitors and professional handlers is that professionals practice their skills day in and day out, in
any ring situation that arises. They have a deep well of visual memories of what winning and the prep that goes
into winning looks and feels like. Let’s estimate that a pro goes in the ring 3 times in a day,3 days a week for 30
weeks a year. That gives the pro almost 300 practices stacking, interacting with the judge and gaiting a dog. What
can we do to enhance our learning, experience, and competitive edge? We can teach ourselves to clearly and
deeply visualize our precise actions and the dog’s responses to increase our ring experience.
The magic ingredient to level the playing field to achieve success is practice. Studies of professional musicians--
pianists especially, have shown that actual, measured time spent in practice leads to more complete mastery than
does innate talent. One study found that the most exceptional pianists practice a minimum of 2 hours every day,
while mediocre students reported daily practice time to be fifteen minutes. Explore the ‘talent versus practice/
work’ concept in, “Talent is Over-rated”, by Geoff Colvin
https://www.amazon.com/Talent-Overrated-Separates-World-Class-Performers-ebook/dp/B001HD8NZ8?

Have you noticed that you are not drawn to go and talk with folks who are always criticizing, complaining, and
blaming negative outcome on others? Negative, losing mental rehearsals contribute to getting more unsatisfactory
results. We intuitively shy away from the downers at dog shows because subconsciously we may know they
negatively affect our day, too. Sarah Regan, Editor at MBG Mindfulness explains, “The idea that we attract what
we put out has clearly been around since ancient times. Like attracts like—even if we're not conscious of it. We're
always "attracting" positivity or negativity based on the energy we're putting out ourselves.” Read more about the
laws of positive outcome at https://www.mindbodygreen.com/articles/the-law-of-attraction-simplified-what-it-is-
and-how-to-use-it
Consummate dog trainer and mentor to many obedience competitors, Julie Hogan (Pride ‘n Joy) gave me the ad-
vice, “Read the obedience rules into a tape recorder, and then listen to that tape—over and over—while you drive to
dog shows.”. Hearing the rules taught me to visualize the tiniest details in the ring and to have a movie running in
my head showing me exactly what to do. Decades later, when my students ask me a ring performance question, I
still hear the rules in my head. Studying the conformation standard sets the foundation for mental rehearsal before
you show your dog.

Top competitors in any sport often thrive on reading visualization stories told by other top winners. No matter the
sport, the ‘head game’ often determines commands the winner’s circle. In his book, Visualization Power, Bill Bodri
finds, “Done consistently, with regularity, visualizing a perfect performance in vivid detail helps you reliably
hard-wire a great performance in your brain. When using visualization techniques, you must vividly picture yourself
performing the skill or routine 100% perfectly and confidently. The most successful type of athletic practice is to
break down a skill or performance into tiny component pieces and work on perfecting those specific chunks before
linking them back together into a larger whole.”
While you are imprinting your visual movie of perfection into your subconscious, it is most important to make
sure the steps you have made second-nature are correct. Denise Flaim’s
“Built to Last: The Basics of Canine Conformation & Anatomy” article has great guidance.
https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/sports/basics-canine-conformation-anatomy/
Sports psychologist coaches ask their athletes to envision an actual perfect performance before they go to sleep at
night and again before their feet hit the floor at the beginning of each new day. Give it a try. Sweet dreams seeing
your gorgeous dog sail around the ring to win.
