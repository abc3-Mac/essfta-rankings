The Adventures of Monroe Riddle
By Steve Earley and Joshua Riddle
E very boy who has read the Adventures of Huckleberry Finn dreams of such a life. No sc hool, no pesky
parents, adventure, living off the land, guns, hunting, fishing, and a loyal companion. My boyhood was the
c omplete opposite growing up in the asphalt jungle of Los Angeles. I did have a loyal friend with whom I
did some deep-sea fishing.
My friend and I dreamed of living in the country where we could roam, hunt and fish and always be ac-
companied by our faithful hunting dogs. Me, in the woods of Maine where my parents were from and he
in Texas where his parents came from. My dog was to be a Britany Spaniel like my Uncle Donald once
owned. Alas, it was just a dream with some parts coming true during adulthood.
But there is one 8-year-old boy near Trenton, UT that is living the dream life of every boy who loves the
o utdoors and dogs. Monroe Riddle is the son of Joshua and Rebecca Riddle. He is oldest of four chil-
dren, Monroe, Carolina (6), Forest (4) and Matilda (1) Monroe’s father and grandfather are dog breeders
and trainers; grandpa full time and his dad part-time.
Their home is out in the county where they kennel some thirty dogs, raise pheasants and train Springer
S paniels and English Cocker Spaniels. He’s there when puppies are born, and pheasants a re hunted. Two
things keep him from being another Huck Finn; his parents who insist he go to school and pesky Carolina
that thinks she’s a cat when she gets mad a Monroe (for no reason of course, he says). But those scratch-
es do take a while to heal up.
Best of all though are his Springer Spaniel “Sharky” and his own 20 ga. O/U shotgun. He puts both to
good use each fall hunting pheasants, ruffed grouse, doves, rabbits, and the occasional waterfowl. He even
runs Sharky in Springer Spaniel field trials competing against seasoned owners and trainers. When not do-
ing all that, he helps his dad and grandpa with the dog training as bird handler and shooter. All this since
he passed his hunter safety test at age six. The instructor said he was the youngest that he had ever
passed.
During the fall of 2022 he had many great hunting trips with his loyal companion Sharky. One hunting trip
was on Ruffed Grouse. Sharky was hunting through the trees and got hot on a bird. He continued to
track the bird up a little ravine into some thicker cover where he produced the bird. Monroe had a little
gap between the pines and quaking aspens and let his shot fly. He was able to connect and brought the
bird down. Sharky was sent on the retrieve and found it quickly.
During the pheasant hunt, he and his dad were pushing a slough where they had seen indications of birds.
It was a difficult year because of drought conditions, and they had not had the success that as in previous
years. They had been able to get a few roosters here and there, but very tough conditions. In this area

On another occasion, accompanying dad and grandpa he was watching for grouse in a new area that they

that he had seen one. Grandpa proceeded to back the truck up and look into the tree that Monroe said
t hat he had seen a bird. Sure enough, standing on a limb about 15’ in the air was a Ruffed Grouse. They
proceeded to get the dogs out and go after it. Monroe was able to get the one in the tre e, and as they
pushed the trees about a half dozen more got up. He was able to get 1 more out of that flock and it
turned into a successful outing.
This past spring, he was able to compete in multiple AKC licensed Field Trials. He ran his dog Sharky all
on his own in these competitions and did very well. Their connection is a very special one and they both
have a mutual respect plus a common goal. One specific event was extra special because he earned a 3rd
place Ribbon. During the event he was called back to all 3 series and was able to put on quite a show.
Sharky ran very well, listened to him and as a team they worked out 6 birds with multiple retrieves and
just some all-around great dog and handler work. There were 23 participating dogs in the event and plac-
ing 3rd was a very big accomplishment for anyone much less an 8-year-old with his dog.
Like we said, Monroe is truly “living the dream”. One that any boy or girl would envy.

Thoughts From The Field
Submitted by Mike Nolan, Field Vice President
The Amateur and the Open Nationals are complete. Nice job by both groups. A round
of applause for the Nebraska group….the fifth series was great…..and the dogs really
showed off. Nice job Colin!!
Speaking of the Nationals, the Parent club has the responsibility of the National Amateur.
The Open is an AKC event. Date of this trial is always being questioned due to weather,
seed, cover travel etc. I want to form a committee to attempt to come up with a viable
method, that can gain consensus, to discuss this issue and bring it to a vote for the
Delegates meeting. If you would like to be on this group please email me
(mnolan4862@comcast.net). I want to limit this group to five or so and fro m different
areas of the US if possible. Please email me your ideas if you are not selecte d for this
group and I promise I will consider them all.
The 100th Parent Club trial is going to be held at the end of October in Elmira NY.
Please consider attending. We hope to have 100 dogs and make this a once every
100year event!!
The seed issue continues to be a problem. I want to make sure we understand the role
the Parent Club plays in this discussion. The Parent Club highly recommends that the
Protocol developed by the Committee on Mean seeds be utilized as the format and pro-
cedure for doing the seed survey before any national. The NOC approved this at their
last delegates meeting. The NAC has tabled it for discussion. We anticipate it being
passed. Every year when the National presentations are made the survey is listed as the
procedure or the survey team is listed. The Parent Club has NO authority to demand
the approved survey be utilized. The Delegates can vote NO. The Delegates have all
the power, we can advise and recommend.

Presidents Message
Submitted by Cristy Joy
Tis the season of gratitude and we have plenty to be grateful for. Each one of you
should be very proud of your contributions to our sports. Whether it was exhibiting
your beautiful dogs in the show ring, participating with your athletes in the perfor-
mance events, judging, or volunteering at club activities, we are grateful for that.
Your commitment and dedication to the English Springer Spaniel breed has helped
our beloved sports continue to flourish.
2023 has been a growing year and I will lead the Parent Club forward in a positive manner. In
the 2nd year of my presidency, I will continue my goals of bringing about communic ation and tak-
ing action in the best interests of the English Springer Spaniel with our membership. Our club
has a rich history of camaraderie and collaboration - let's work together to celebrate our similari-
ties and treasure our
differences!
2024 will be a packed year and I look forward to the work ahead. I am pleased to s ee us meeting
goals and continually making progress, month after month. We will recognize the completion of
our Breed Illustrated Standard, being illustrated by one of our very own talented members. This
spring we will be developing and launching a new website. In October we will celebrate the 100th
anniversary of ESS Field Trials. The Field Trail Apprentice program and the Bench Judge’s Edu-
cation committees are hard at work developing valuable resources for the English Springer
Spaniel programs.
The English Springer Spaniel community is a family, and we are deeply grateful that you
are a part of it. Your hard work and devotion to our breed are key parts of what
makes the English Springer Spaniel Parent Club great. With your help, we will con-
tinue to be one of the most prestigious clubs in the United States. Stay true to our
breed!
We wish you a happy, healthy, and peaceful Holiday Season and a prosperous and
joyous New Year!!!
Go Springers!!

E NGLISH SPRINGER SPANIEL FOUNDATION
Submitted by: Francie Nelson
T his is a request for your support of a specific, high priority project – our continuing study of Progressive Reti-
n al Atrophy. The Springer community has consistently expressed its concern about PRA and its desire and sup-
port for answers. You have participated generously in the first phase of research. We ask for your financial sup-
port as we begin the second phase.
This year, we closed out our Phase 1 study on Progressive Retinal Atrophy and reported our findings at the
2023 National Specialty in October at the Purina Event Center. The turnout was a testament to the passionate
interest the Springer community has in improving the health and quality of life of our dogs.
Dr. Miyadera reported that at the conclusion of her four year study she found the following:
1. Advanced diagnostic techniques (such as ERG) demonstrate that most if not all genetically
affected dogs are both affected and afflicted.
2. Extensive genetic analysis has not identified a conclusive modifier/variant gene.
3. It is not currently possible to tell whether a genetically affected dog will end up on the mild or severe end of
the spectrum.
4. It is still recommended to move away from the RPGRIP1 gene variant over time; however, it is
not recommended to suddenly eliminate all carriers or even affecteds from the breeding gene pool.
5. RPGRIP1-PRA testing remains valid for English Springer Spaniels.
This information is a valuable tool for owners and breeders alike as they make important decisions about their
breeding programs and managing quality of life. We need to continue this work to better understand what fac-
tors are influencing the progression of the disease, why littermates show significantly different rates of disease
progression, and how we might look for controllable factors or even interventions.
Will you help us as we embark on an ambitious Phase 2 study? Your continued support in bringing your dogs
for the study is invaluable, and your financial donations are necessary to assure we can continue this very im-
portant work.
On behalf of English Springer Spaniel friends and fans everywhere, thank you!

Cathy must receive all materials no later than January 15, 2023.

Nominations by Petition. Additional nominations of eligible members for the Board may be made by written petition ad-
dressed to the Membership Secretary and received at his regular address on or before January 15th, signed by members of the
Club equal to or in excess of 10% of the Club’s membership, and accompanied by the written acceptance of each such additional
nominee signifying his willingness to be a candidate. No person shall be a candidate for more than one position; and a candidate
for the Board must specify whether he is running for (i) a Bench Regional Governorship post, (ii) a Field Regional Governorship
post, (iii) an At Large Governorship post, or (iv) one of the foregoing, plus an officership post. If a candidate by petition runs for a
Board post for which the Nominating Committee’s nominee (or one of its nominees) is also the Committee’s nominee for an offic-
ership post, such candidate must also run for the same officership post, in the manner set out below, if he desires to be treated as
being in contention with the Committee’s Governor/officer nominee. Additional nominations for any officership position may be
made by written petition addressed to the Corresponding Secretary, signed by three members of the Board of Governors (as con-
stituted as of the date of the Petition), and otherwise in accordance with the foregoing provisions applicable to a petition for a
nominee for the Board. Additional nominations which are provided for herein may be made only from among those members who
have not accepted a nomination of the Nominating Committee.
Uncontested Elections. If no valid additional nominations for Board positions are received by the Membership Secretary
on or before January 15th, nominations shall close, and the Nominating Committee’s slate shall be declared elected at the time of
the Annual Meeting of members, with no balloting required. If no valid additional nominations for officership positions are received
by the Corresponding Secretary on or before January 15th, nominations shall close, and the Nominating Committee’s slate shall
be declared elected at the time of the Annual Meeting of the Board, with no balloting required.
Contested Elections. If one or more valid additional nominations for the Board are received by the Membership Secretary
on or before January 15th, he shall, on or before March 15th, distribute electronically to those members having declared ac-
ceptance of electronic communications, and by First Class Mail to each member who designates transmission of Ballots via First
Class Mail, in good standing a ballot listing all of the nominees in alphabetical order, with the names of the States in which they
reside, together with a signature envelope and a return envelope addressed to the Membership Secretary to provide for a secret
voting process. The Tellers of election shall check the returns against the list of members whose dues are paid for the current
year, and they shall certify the eligibility of the voters as well as the results of the voting which shall be announced at the Annual
Meeting of members. Cumulative voting shall be and is prohibited. If one or more valid additional nominations for the officership
positions are received by the Corresponding Secretary on or before January 15th, he shall, on or before March 15th, mail to each
Governor a ballot listing all of the nominees in alphabetical order, with the names of the States in which they reside, together with
a signature envelope and a return envelope addressed to the Corresponding Secretary. In addition, prior to March 15th, the Cor-
responding Secretary shall provide the Membership Secretary with a list of any additional nomination(s) for officership positions,
which the latter Secretary shall mail to the members.
