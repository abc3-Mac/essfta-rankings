The President’s Message
Submitted by Liz Pike
Happy Fall! I hope all of you have had an opportunity over the summer to enjoy activities with your Springers
and prepare for the fall field trial and show season.
Recognizing members for their contributions is important to honoring those who have had a great influence on
both our sport and our people. The AKC Outstanding Sportsmanship and the Lifetime Achievement committees
received and evaluated nominations for these prestigious awards and presented the A KC Outstanding
Sportsmanship award to Laureen Camisi and our first ever Lifetime Achievement awards to Randy Capsel,
Betsy Davis, Julia Gasow (posthumous), Laurie Green, Bonnie Regan, and Hen riette Schmidt.
Awards for our Field Lifetime Achievement honorees will be presented at the National Amateur in December.
Please congratulate these wonderful honorees.
The Board of Governors and the committees are continuing to make progress on critical initi atives- one of our
key areas of focus is education. Videos from the Field Trial Advanced Judging Conference and the Gunning
seminar have been posted to the website; presentations at the National Specialty from both Breeders Ed (video)
and an interactive Judge’s Ed presentation have also been made available. Another area of focus is completing and
publishing the Illustrated Standard, clarifying the various aspects of the Field Trial judge’s Apprentice program, and
updating the Blue Book. We also have geared up our plans for the 2026 Centennial celebration of the recognition
of the ESSFTA as the Parent Club of English Springer Spaniels – it will be a fun, nostalgic, and exciting year for us!
I want to devote the remainder of my message to the updated AKC Code of Sportsmanship and Civility.
With the addition of the word Civility AKC has added seven new descriptors of their expecta tions for us when
participating in AKC events or other activities within our community. I’ve highlighted the new phrases and
encourage everyone to carefully read them and embrace these values of fairness and respect – our fellow
competitors, judges, officials, volunteers, spectators, and AKC staff all deserve to be treated with courtesy both in
person and on social media. It would be naïve to think that we can stop all criticism (we all live in glass houses)
but I would encourage all of us to think more deeply before sharing those thoughts.
It may also be worth noting that AKC will exercise jurisdiction over complaints related to violation of the Code of
Sportsmanship and Civility. If you read the AKC Gazette and peruse the Secretary’s Page, you will see AKC
Imposing fines and suspensions based on violations of the Code – so while I hope the tenents of the Code are
embraced by most simply because it is the right thing to do, AKC has put some teeth into these expectations.
We are fortunate to have members that demonstrate these values every day and I am grateful to all of you for
your continued commitment to the ESSFTA.
Enjoy your Springers!
Liz

2025 National Specialty Report
Submitted by President, Liz Pike
And It’s a Wrap! 2025 National Specialty
The 2025 National Specialty was a huge success – celebratory, exhausting, and a true joy. With t he addition of
the two regional specialties prior to the National Specialty conformation shows, a weekend prior filled with scent
work trials, educational presentations, two agility, obedience, and rally trials, the Top 20 celebration, the ESRA
25th Anniversary event, and the many “fun events”, there was something for everyone. And, mos t importantly, it
was an opportunity to meet new people and reconnect with old friends!
When you hear the phrase, “it takes a village”, I have no doubt that a National Specialty is a perfect example of it,
starting with the best committee you could possibly put together:
Cathy Brooks (Merchandise), Kim Bullard (Junior and Pee Wee events), Grady Dunham (RV Parking),
Ashley Dornak (Rosettes, Trophies and Grooming), Laura Edwards (Welcome Bags), Ginge r Huxley and
Cathy Pullian (scent work, CGC/Tricks and “Show Me Awards), the indefatigable Laurie Green (Regional
Specialties and Top 20), Courtney Hansen (Rosettes, Trophies, Agility, Rally and Obedience), Sue Ellen Healy
(Judge Liaison), Erin Kerfoot (Judges Ed), Patty Mortara (website, Facebook, all around proje ct manager ex-
traordinaire), Polly Mumma (Awards Celebration), Liz Purkaple (Treasurer), Sue Schroer (Rescue Events),
Sarah Wilms (Breeders Ed) and the extraordinary Kathy Patregnani (Co-chair, Agility, Ra lly, Obedience,
Vendors, Ice cream scooping, hospitality, etc.!). These devoted volunteers worked tirelessly throughout
the year to bring you this amazing event – please join me in thanking them!
In addition to the committees, we are grateful to all those who stepped up to volunteer at the National Specialty –
stewards: Nancy Johnson (head ring steward), Cathy Ross, Sarah Wilms, Cindy Pulliam and Tracey Thompson,
Show Central (these folks generally signed up for a couple hours and stayed for many more!) : Christi Cooper,
Brenda Bateman, Deb Evans, Jill Hibbard, Sally Breck and many more who wandered in and were put to work!
Agility Chief course builders – Jen Carl Howard and Jenny Kroll, Agility, Obedience and Rally volunteers Ginny
Mohr, Toni Alden and Laura Burns. We were fortunate to have Dr. Keiko Miyadera with us again this year for
the continuation of our PRA research project as well as Tim Pullian and Patty Meiser who ran the DNA bank
blood draw. Special thanks to our show secretary, Lorraine Yu, who went above and beyond her job to even as-
sist at Show Central. We are especially grateful to Cathy Flanders who helped in Show Central, conducted CGC
testing, spearheaded fundraising for the Lifetime Achievement Award, and managed the Parent Club meeting - she
is just amazing! We also thank our own “rescue crews” – Kate Bremser took care of both a member and her dogs
that were in a car accident. Grady and Jill Dunham and Al and Terra Collver rescued Kathy Patregnani, Courtney
and eight dogs when Kathy Patregnani’s RV broke down. Our dog community is the best! If we omitted any-
one from these lists, mia culpa! – it was an oversight – we truly appreciate everyone who helped.
We are already in the process of planning for our National Specialty in 2026 in collaboration with two great clubs
in the pacific northwest – Puget Sound ESSA and Willamette Valley ESSA. If you are interested in helping, please
reach out to either Liz Pike or Kathy Patregnani – we would welcome you. See you in Albany, Oregon October
12 to October 17, 2026!

New Governor to Join The Board
Submitted by President, Liz Pike
We have had a change in the Board of Governors. Beth Fink, Bench Regional Governor, has ten dered her
resignation from the board. In accordance with the Bylaws the BOG has elected to fill the vacancy for the unexpired
portion of the term which will be complete at the annual meeting in 2026.
SECTION 4. Vacancies. Any vacancy occurring in the Board of Governors may be filled by the Board. Each
Governor appointed to fill a vacancy shall hold office for the unexpired term of his predecesso r. In addition, in
the case of a vacancy in the office of a Regional Governor, the President may designate an incumbent
Governor to serve as an “Acting Regional Governor” respecting the Region to which the vacan cy relates, such
“Acting Regional Governor” to serve only until the vacancy is otherwise filled by the Board or by the normal
election process.
The BOG has asked Cindy Lester to fill the unexpired term. She will serve as an At-Large Bench governor until the
annual meeting and will be eligible to be nominated to a full three year term if presented by the nominating commit-
tee for that role. Cindy previously served a single three year term from 2020 to 2023 and we are excited to have
her passion and experience back on the Board of Governors. Attached is Cindy's bio - please welcome her to the
ESSFTA BOG.
Cindy Lester — Bench At Large Governor
My love of dogs brought me to where I am today.
I have shown English Springer Spaniels for 20 years and completed Championships on several of my own breed-
ings. I have had the pleasure of judging several all-breed matches; two English Springer Spaniel and one Brittany
sweepstakes. Two years ago I started competitive obedience and scent work.
In 2010, I acquired my foundation bitch from my mentor and whelped my first litter in 2010. I continue to study the
breed and keep in touch with the majority of our puppy families. Seeing photos and hearing stories is extremely
rewarding.
My background is in Anatomical Pathology which has proven valuable in understanding various health issues in
canines. The later part of my career, I was in management which helped me with people skills such as listening and
communicating.
I currently work for the Saccarappa Obedience Club as an AKC STAR PUPPY, CGC and Obedience 101 instructor
and I love it! I also teach conformation handling classes on my own at a nearby training facility.
I’ve stayed active in the dog show fancy since my last time as a governor. I was invited to join the Breeder Educa-
tion Committee, became a member of the Keystone English Springer Spaniel Club and I was awarded an Honorary
membership to the Saccarappa Obedience Club. I continue to show my dogs in conformation and do scent work.
My goal is to work with the youngsters who show interest in conformation and pet obedience.
As a Governor, I will encourage local fanciers to share ideas and hopefully get more people involved in ESSFTA.

National Specialty Report
Submitted by Bench VP, Kim Bullard
We just finished the ESSFTA National Specialty, and it was another fabulous week of beautiful Sp ringers and their
people. The National Specialty is always a highlight of my year, getting to see beautiful dogs and wonderful people
that I typically only get to see once a year. If you attended, I hope you also had a great time and we really hope
you plan to visit us in beautiful Albany, Oregon in October next year!
The Judges Ed and Breeders Ed committees continue to make progress. If you missed the Judges Education and/or
Breeders education, you really did miss a great event! The Breeders Education workshop was filmed and can be
found here: 2025 ESSFTA Breeder Education.
The ESSFTA meeting held at the National Specialty had some technical difficulties regarding the Z oom, but we
appreciate all who attended not only in person but virtually.
Also, if you missed the update, we received the final decision from AKC on making English Springer Spaniels a
ramp mandatory breed. This will go into effect Jan. 1, 2026.
Juniors/Peewees at the National 2025
On behalf of not only the ESSFTA Juniors Committee but all the juniors and peewees that were at the 2025
ESSFTA National specialty I want to truly thank all who donated to make another year of AMAZING Junior
Goodie bags!! I tell you, every year I admittedly agree these kids are spoiled! We try our best to keep the
expense of these Goodie bags to only what is donated. I will admit, I was really worried initially with the lack of
donations but you all quickly let that worry subside. The bags themselves are stunning, and then the donors that
either purchased from the Amazon Wishlist or donated at the event really filled them. Susie Hatch and Hydra
donated full size bottles of Hydra products as well!
Friday afternoon shortly after bitch judging we had six adorable PeeWees step into the ring to strut their stuff,
and this truly is a highlight of my week. Thank you, Janet Warner, for volunteering your time to “judge” the pee-
wees and Congelton photos for graciously coming to photograph as well. Saturday morning judging started with a
lovely and talented group of Junior Handlers and not to mention Juniors in Performance events too. We should
be very proud of these young people in our sport.

The Committee will operate under the following “Best Practices” guidelines:
Committee Best Practices
English Springer Spaniel Field Trial Association
Standing committees and task forces are the lifeblood of the organization and the volunteers in those groups are
vital to advancing the objectives of the Parent Club.
The President, Executive Committee or the Board of Governors may each year appoint standing committees to
advance the work of the Club in such matters as dog shows, performance events, field trials, trophies, annual
prizes, membership and other initiatives which may well be served by committees. Special committees/ task
forces may also be appointed to aid it in particular projects. All committees are always subject to the final
authority of the Board of Governors.
The Board of Governors provides oversight for committees/task forces and as such, provides the following Best
Practices for ESSFTA Committees and Task Forces:
1. Committee chairpersons are responsible for defining the mission of the committee and responsibilities,
authority, and expectations for committee members; this information must be sent to the Corresponding
Secretary for publication on the ESSFTA website.
2. Lists of committee chairpersons and committee members must be updated annually prior to the annual Parent
Club meeting; chairpersons are responsible for providing that information to the Corresponding Secretary. This
updated list will be reviewed by the BOG at the Parent Club Annual Meeting.
3. At such time as a Committees and/or Task Forces has concluded their work and are no longer active, the
Chairperson must report this to the Executive Committee and the Corresponding Secretary for updating on the
ESSFTA website.
4. Chairpersons should strive to recruit committee members with relevant expertise and diverse opinions. All
chairs and committee members must be ESSFTA members in good standing.
5. Rotation of committee members should take into account a balance between providing opportunity and fresh
thinking with the need for continuity and historical perspective.

Judge's Education Committee (Conformation) - At the very core, conformation dog shows
are designed to evaluate breeding stock. The health and wellbeing of our breed are tied to systems by which the
potential of individual dogs to serve future generations as breeding stock is identified. Judges’ opinions are
sought, and a value is placed upon these evaluations.
Judges Education provides support and educational experiences for current and prospective judges. Seminars
offered by breed clubs typically provide a venue in which those who want to become judges engage in an in-
depth discussion of traits outlined in a Breed Standard, observation of dogs, discussion with breed experts, hands
-on practice examining dogs, and writing critiques of the dogs they examined.
The Judges Education Committee is comprised of a Steering Committee and Sub-Committees / Task Forces. A
steering committee provides high-level oversight and strategic guidance for a project, often with decision-making
authority. The Steering Committee includes the chair and co-chair of the specific sub-committee / task force and
the ESSFTA officers and Board of Governors. A sub-committee / task force is a smaller group of members as-
sembled to address a specific objective, project, goal, or complete a focused task. Essentially, a steering commit-
tee sets the direction while a task force does the hands-on work to achieve it. Together they play a vital role in
educating and mentoring individuals who want to become judges, know more about the English Springer Spaniel
breed, and those who want to know more about the judging process. The committee presents at AKC judges’
education seminars, other judges’ groups that request a program, the National Specialty, and regional clubs that
invite them to present at their specialties. They are responsible for developing educational materials, producing
the ESSFTA Breed Illustrated Standard, and training the Judges’ Education presenters and Ringside Mentors. The
chairman of the Judges Education Committee oversees all Sub-Committees and Task Forces.

Working Dog/Hunting Committee - The ESSFTA Working Dog/Hunting Committee oversees
the ESSFTA Working Dog program and associated records, provides support, oversight, and manages finances
for AKC spaniel hunting tests conducted under the ESSFTA license.
The chairperson, or delegate, approves requests from clubs requesting to host a spaniel working dog test and
wanting to include English Springer Spaniels as part of the test. The committee ensures working dog test forms
and guidelines are updated and provided to requesting clubs. Once a working dog test is completed the host club
will submit completed paperwork/score sheets to the committee. The committee is then responsible for issuing
certificates to those English Springer Spaniels earning a Working Dog (WD) or Working Dog Excellent (WDX)
title. A database is maintained by the committee for all English Springer Spaniels earning a WD or WDX title and
this information is regularly updated onto the ESSFTA website.
The committee also assists the National Specialty Committee with planning proposed AKC spaniel hunting tests
conducted in conjunction with the National Specialty. This support is either provided directly by the committee
in conducting the test or providing financial support and guidance to a local club hosting the hunting test. The
committee also oversees the ESSFTA Master Hunting Dog Excellent program, an ESSFTA specific titling event,
should requests be submitted from a club wishing to host an event.
The committee maintains a specific checking account for the ESSFTA Hunting Committee for which funds can be
utilized for the WD Program or ESSFTA spaniel hunting tests. The committee also provides the Board of
Governors an annual activity report and a budget summary of the past year along with a proposed budget for the
upcoming year.

Mentors in Judges’ Education
Mentors play a critical role in shaping the understanding and consistency of breed judging. The American
Kennel Club (AKC) outlines clear expectations for mentors to ensure credibility, professionalism, and
educational value.
Establishing Criteria
Parent clubs are encouraged to adopt transparent standards when selecting mentors. At a minimum, the
AKC recognizes either a parent club–approved mentor or an individual with at least 12 ye ars of experience
exhibiting or judging the breed in conformation. Many clubs, however, choose to go further by requiring:
Years of active involvement in breeding, showing, or performance events
Longstanding club membership
Breeding achievements, such as producing champions or titled dogs
Geographic accessibility and availability to mentor
Some clubs may require mentors to pass breed exams, anatomy tests, or demonstrate knowledge of
breed-specific disqualifications (e.g., height or weight checks). Workshops are often used to train mentors
in their role, outlining expectations, mentoring opportunities, and AKC form procedures.
ESSFTA Judges’ Education Committee
The ESSFTA’s Judges’ Education Committee is comprised of four subcommittees: Seminar & Workshop,
Virtual Breed Course, Breed Illustrated Standard & Educational Materials, and Ringside Mentoring &
Tutoring. Together, they ensure that aspiring judges, and anyone seeking to deepen their understanding of
the English Springer Spaniel, have access to reliable resources, accurate instruction, and strong mentor-
ship.

Brackets Formula
By the early 1950's, Lloyd C. Brackett had become a legend in his own time. In part because of the
quality of the dogs he produced and in part because of his candor when addressing problems related to
the breeding of canines. He had much to say about the selection of sires, how to correct problems and
how to make improvements. Brackett was considered one of the fathers of the German Shepherd breed
in the United States. At the time of his death he was the oldest living continuos fancier of the breed
(since 1912). His kennel was called Long Worth and he is remembered throughout the d og world for his
theories about breeding methods. Brackett was well read and a quick learner. Through his writings he
shed light on the confusion and misunderstandings associated with line and inbreeding. One of his
greatest achievements was to have produced over 90 champions in twelve years.
All of his methods and ideas were not new. For example, he combined the study of pedigrees with the
results they produced. After years of watching what combinations produced the better offspring he
refined his ideas about how to select breeding partners. Out of these experiences came a formula that
later he would make him famous. The formula was not new but his ideas about how to use it attracted
attention. Breeders of domestic animals had used it for years. It relied on the principles of line and
inbreeding. But it was Brackett and his approach to planned breedings that made it well known. Brackett
believed in pedigree analysis, litter evaluation, the use of line and inbreeding and a record system that
was easy to use. Those ideas are what set him apart from others who did little more than practice the art
of breeding. While Brackett is best known for his emphasis on the use of line breeding he was not afraid
to inbreed if the situation dictated it. Brackett believed that it made no sense to go forward with breeding
before the needed information about the sire and dam had been collected. He placed great emphasis on
health, temperament and breed characteristics. His planned breedings were based on the results that
occurred in his pups. In other words, he learned from his mistakes.
Brackett understood the value of using quality dogs that were related to each other. This approach
allowed him to concentrate the genes needed to produce desired traits. His techniques for reducing
error and improving quality focused on the careful selection of breeding partners. They were central to
maintaining and improving specific traits while at the same time reducing disease and other unwanted
problems. Brackett became famous for breeding quality dogs with consistent type. His strategy relied on
a series of breedings using relatives. Often times he was quoted as saying, "never outcross when things
seem to be going well, do it only as an experiment or when some fault or faults cannot be eliminated".
He was careful to study each stud dog and their offspring, eliminating those who did not measure up
and those who produced faults. Close inspection of his pedigrees show that many of his sires were
themselves inbred or line bred and most were usually related in some way to the bitches in his breeding

It has been well documented that two full-brothers usually do not have the same genet ic potential even
though they both come from the same two parents. One sibling might inherit one set of genes from his
father and the other might get a different set from an uncle through his mother. While each pup always

same set of genes. This explains why littermates do not always look alike or have the same capacity to
produce quality. Brackett kept detailed records on the differences between siblings. He was well read on
this subject and occasionally mentioned the works of Aristotle and Mendel in his articles . In practice they
all shared similar beliefs.
Brackett was usually quick to comment on what he observed and how things could be improved. In a
statement taken from one of his articles, he said, "whenever two or three dog fanciers get together there
is almost sure to be talk about linebreeding. The term may be used without anyone of th em having a real
understanding of what it means. There seems to be much confusion even in the minds of experienced
dog breeders about the actual meaning of the terms and how to differentiate between them". He referred
to this dilemma in several articles in a variety of scenarios. He once raised several questions when he
heard two breeders discussing a line breeding. He referred to the breeder who recommended it with the
statement, linebred to what? He knew that the answer to the question would be a measure of what the
breeder actually knew about the term. It was his way of evaluating the wisdom of others. He knew that
line breeding can mean many things. For example, a dog can be line bred on its sire's side of the
pedigree or on its dam's side. Those who use the term usually understand it to mean only that the dogs
are related to each other.
Brackett was concerned about the future of breeding better dogs and the lack of breeder education
programs. He believed that "the majority of dog breeders formulate no breeding plan and seldom if ever,
when making a mating consider how or what they will mate any of the resultant progeny."

Formula Variations
Breeders quickly learned that variations could be made in Brackett's preferred formula based on the strengths and
weaknesses of the bitch. While they were not as productive as the preferred formula they did work to concentrate the
genes needed. The variations of the formula can be stated as follows: Let the sire of the sire be the grandsire of the
dam on the sire's side instead of on the dam's side. Another variation let the sire be the result of either a full or half
brother and sister mating and thus inbred. In each case selecting a mate for a faulty bitch such as "A" whose
wide-open pedigree offers no strength would not be a good use of these formulas.
The selection of breeding partners must always focus on correcting weaknesses in pedigrees and making
improvements. To do other wise is a waste of time.

Immune Mediated Hemolytic Anemia (IMHA)
IMHA is an autoimmune disease in dogs in which the body attacks its own read blood cells. This can
lead to low red blood cell counts (anemia) and can predispose dogs to forming blood clots in the lungs or
brain. Treatment with transfusions and drugs to suppress the immune system is often effective but for
other dogs, the disease is fatal. It has been found that IMHA is disproportionately found in four spaniel
breeds, including English Springer Spaniels.

to see if you have a dog that could qualify for the study, please visit his lab site for more information:

Paroxysmal Dyskinesia (PD)
Paroxysmal Dyskinesia is an episodic, involuntary movement disorder that is often misdiagnosed as
idiopathic seizure or epilepsy. Affected dogs will exhibit involuntary movements of one or more limbs
during the event which may be brief and mild or may occur in clusters and last for hours. One of the
distinguishing factors for PD is that the dog remains fully alert and post-episode recovery is almost im-
mediate. PD appears to be self-limiting and dogs often simply stop having episodes.
While PD may occur in a number of breeds, specific, recognized forms of PD have been identified in
Cavalier King Charles Spaniels, Labrador Retrievers and several terrier breeds. Genetic tests are
available for Cavaliers, Soft Coated Wheaten Terriers, Shetland Sheepdogs, and Weimaraners with
studies underway for a number of other breeds to identify the causative mutation.
There is some evidence that PD exists in the ESS population and may be found more typically in some
families of dogs. Interested owners can submit samples to the University of Missouri and Dr. Martin
Katz at katzm@health.missoouri.edu.

integrity and exemplify fairness and honesty. He or she must be courteous, kind and friendly, especially to newcomers. He
or she promotes goodwill and encourages others when encouragement is required. He or she offers ideas to others for
breeding, care, grooming, handling, competing, training, etc. He or she is a role model for sportsmanship, and a kind and
generous mentor. He or she congratulates and supports the wins and accomplishments of others and offers good wishes.

The health testing requirements for Show Dog/Bitch and Show Sire/
Dam and all runners up were updated as of the 2024 Award year.
The verbiage for Show Sire/ Show Dam, Show Dog/Show Bitch as well as all
runner ups has changed to reflect the current policy. The policy now reads:
Health Clearances
Award winners for Show Sire/ Show Dam, Show Dog/Show Bitch as well as all runner
ups are required to have completed all CHIC Health Clearances (hips, elbows,
CAER eye exam, PRA test and submission to the OFA DNA bank) and have results

CHIC strategy of transparency, all results must be publicly available (through OFA)
but there are no specific requirements for test results.
If you have a dog/bitch that might be a contender for these awards in 2025, please make sure
to have these health tests completed by the end of the year!
