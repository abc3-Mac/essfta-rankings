The President’s Message
Submitted by Cristy Joy
My Love for English Springer Spaniels and Competing in AKC Field Trials
As I come home from spending time at the 2024 National Amateur Championship, I have reflected on why
I am dedicated to the ESSFTA, our Parent Club. English Springer Spaniels are not just dogs; they are loyal
companions, skilled athletes, and, for me, my heart and soul. My journey with this remarkable breed has
been one filled with passion, excitement, and a deep bond that transcends the typical owner-pet relationship.
As I reflect on my experiences, I want to share why I am so drawn to English Springer Span iels and what
it’s like to compete in the prestigious AKC (American Kennel Club) field trials with them.
A Breed Like No Other
English Springer Spaniels are a special breed, known for their keen intelligence, boundless energy, and ver-
satility. They excel in various fields, particularly as hunting dogs, where their natural instinct to retrieve and
work in the field truly shines. These dogs are incredibly responsive, trainable, and always eager to please,
which makes them ideal partners for someone like me, who loves the challenge of competin g and working
closely with a dog in the field.
Their affectionate and friendly nature also makes them the perfect family companion. However, when it
comes to field trials, it’s their hunting instincts, stamina, and drive that take center stage. Whether they’re
flushing birds from dense cover or sprinting through the open field, their commitment and excitement are
contagious. I find immense joy in watching them at work, and it’s this bond of teamwork th at has fueled my
love for field trials.
The Thrill of AKC Field Trials
Competing in AKC field trials has been one of the most exhilarating and rewarding experiences of my life.
Field trials are competitive events where dogs showcase their hunting skills in realistic field settings. The
goal is to demonstrate a dog’s ability to work independently, respond to commands, and display proper
fieldwork etiquette, all while remaining focused and efficient.
For me, these trials are not just about winning ribbons or trophies; it’s about the connection between me and
my English Springer Spaniel. Each run through the field is a test of our teamwork, understanding, and train-
ing. The thrill of watching my dog retrieve a bird after a well-executed flush, or the pride I feel when we
successfully complete a trial, is unmatched. These moments are a testament to the hours of training, pa-
tience, and trust we’ve built together.
Training and Preparation
Training an English Springer Spaniel for field trials is no easy task, but the rewards are well worth the ef-
fort. It requires a combination of patience, consistency, and a deep understanding of the dog’s instincts. This
is done through positive reinforcement, repetition, and gradual exposure to real-world hunting situations.
An essential part of training is making sure the dog can work independently, yet remain responsive to com-
mands. In the field, a well-trained English Springer Spaniel will be able to navigate through different ter-
rains, track scent, and flush birds at the right time—all while maintaining focus and control. This level of
preparation is critical for success in AKC field trials, where precision and timing are everything.

Why I Keep Coming Back
As much as the competition itself drives me, it’s the love of the breed and the camaraderie of the sport
that keeps me coming back. Each trial offers new challenges and opportunities for growth —both for me
as a handler and for my English Springer Spaniel as a working dog. The sense of accomplishment that
comes from seeing our hard work pay off is a feeling like no other. It’s not just about the accolades; it’s
about the deep connection I share with my dog, the respect we’ve earned for each other, and the thrill of
competing in such a prestigious event.
English Springer Spaniels have taught me so much over the years—not just about hunting , but about
patience, perseverance, and the joy of living in the moment. I look forward to many more seasons of

for this amazing breed.

The 2024 ENGLISH SPRINGER SPANIEL
NATIONAL AMATEUR CHAMPIONSHIP
Submitted by Cristy Joy
The 2024 English Springer Spaniel National Amateur Championship (NAC) showcased t he pinnacle of
amateur field trial competition, bringing together passionate handlers, talented dogs, and a shared love for
the breed’s remarkable hunting abilities. Hosted at Pyramid State Park by Pinckneyville Illinois Field
Grounds, this year’s championship was a testament to the enduring spirit and versatility o f the English
Springer Spaniel.
A PRESTIGIOUS EVENT FOR DEDICATED ENTHUSIASTS
The NAC is a premier event within the field trial community, offering amateur handlers the opportunity
to demonstrate their dogs’ skill, obedience, and natural talent in the field. Organized unde r the auspices of
the English Springer Spaniel Parent Club, this annual competition draws participants and spectators from
across the country and Canada.
In 2024, the championship welcomed 116 entries, each representing countless hours of training and prep-
aration. The event not only celebrated the competitors’ achievements but also fostered camaraderie
among Springer enthusiasts.
A CHALLENGING AND REWARDING COURSE
This year’s competition had Judges Chad Betts and Dan Lussen assessing the Handler and English
Springer Spaniels performance. The event featured a course that tested the dogs’ abilities in upland hunt-
ing scenarios. From retrieving birds in varied terrain to demonstrating precision in quarte ring and mark-
ing, every aspect of the championship reflected the English Springer Spaniel’s heritage as a versatile
hunting companion.

Dr Keiko Miyadera Goes to the Nationals
Advancing Springer Health
Submitted by Cristy Joy
The English Springer Spaniel (ESS) Field Trial community continued to support the English
Springer Spaniel Foundation Study into the genetics of PRA (Progressive Retinal Atrophy)
endorsed by the ESSFTA Parent Club. This event continued the amazing particip ation we have
seen at the previous ESSFTA National Specialties.
Dr. Keiko Miyadera, a veterinary ophthalmologist and genetic researcher at the University of
Michigan, in collaboration with the ESS Foundation and AKC CHF, offered complimentary PRA
vision tests and genetic testing as part of this research project at the ESS Nation al Open
Championship and the ESSFTA National Amateur Championship, December 9th through the
19th (is this the 10th?) at Pyramid Park near Pinckneyville, Illinois. The testing included eye
exams, which required dilation, and blood draws during the NAC and NOC event s.
The current focus of the study is to identify genetically affected dogs, related carriers, and those
that have not yet been tested. By clarifying the role of the RPGRIP1 mutation in E SS retinal
disease, this study aims to develop a reliable DNA testing platform, facilitating better detection,
prevention, and breeding strategies to safeguard the breed's future. The advancements in
molecular techniques have identified nearly 30 gene mutations responsible for in herited retinal
diseases in dogs which often lead to vision loss and PRA is of the greatest importance to our
breed. The focus of Dr. Keiko Miyadera’s study involves the following:
Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA) is an inherited degenerative disease that affects the
photoreceptor cells of the retina. In our Phase 1 study, led by our research partner, Dr.
Keiko Miyadera, findings indicated that most, if not all, genetically affected dogs exhibit
both retinal changes and visual impairments. However, the progression of PRA varies
significantly among affected dogs.
The ongoing Phase 2 study aims to identify the factors that influence the rate of disease
progression. PRA disproportionately impacts English Springer Spaniels, and while
genetic testing coupled with breeding practices has reduced the occurrence of afflicted
dogs, there is still much work to be done. By participating in this study, you will contribute
to the health and well-being of future generations of our beloved breed.
A special thank you to Connie Kieckhefer for making this happen. Thank you so much to all who
worked hard in this endeavor, and thank you for your support of this important project.
Together, we can make a meaningful impact on the future of the English Springer Spaniel.

Judges Ed, Illustrated Standard and Breeders Ed Update
The Judges’ Ed committee has continued to refine the teaching materials based on previous feedback
and using the new illustrations from the Breed Illustrated Standard as they become available. The
presentation at the 2024 National Specialty had a great attendance with 32 attendees a nd seven
individuals participating in the hands on workshop. The post presentation survey showed an overall
satisfaction score of 3.92 out of 4, indicating a very high level of satisfaction.
Erin Kerfoot, Judges Education Chairperson, has provided a detailed update on Judges’ Education,
committee information, and future plans; that article follows this report and is full of valuable information.
Take a few minutes to read it – we’re proud of the progress to date and are excited about future plans.
Progress on the Illustrated Standard continues with the next set of drawings has gone to membership,
and we are asking for feedback. The final drawings are perhaps some of the most diffic ult as we seek
to have the right materials to teach our judges about the all-important English Springer Spaniel head.
Fun Fact: The ESS AKC Standard is among the longest; our standard has 2138 words and 544 (over a
quarter of the total description) are used to describe the head!
The “Finding Your Balance” Breeders Ed workshop at the National Specialty had excelle nt participation
and very positive feedback. The Breeders’ Ed Committee is exploring additional educational opportuni-
ties including legacy breeder interviews.
General Updates
The AKC English Springer Spaniel Health statement has been updated and approved by the Board of
Governors to align with our CHIC requirements. This will clarify the requirements for the Breeder of
Merit designation on the AKC site. The updated Health statement has been posted on the AKC.org
website.
https://s3.amazonaws.com/cdn-origin-etr.akc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/20081544/ESSFTA-
Health-Statement-2024-Final.pdf
With AKC lifting the restrictions on Sweepstakes judges, a Sweepstakes judge would be allowed to en-
ter regular classes on the same day/same show and National Specialty is considered a single show.
Individual clubs are permitted to continue to restrict Sweepstakes judges from showing in the regular
classes. The Bench Governors discussed this and determined that because judging at a National is a
huge honor, we would not allow Sweepstakes judges to show in the regular classes at the National
Specialty. Sweepstakes judges for our National Specialties in 2025, 2026, and 2027 will be informed of
this decision.
According to the guidance in the National Specialty Handbook, voting for National Specialty judges
should be done on the even years and would normally have been done in 2024. Because we were es-
sentially “ahead” due to both the cancelation of the 2020 National Specialty and the substitution for our
2023 judge, the Bench Governors recommended that we delay voting until late in 2025 or early 2026.
We have judges committed for 2025, 2026 and 2027; this voting would be for our 2028 and 2029
judges.

ESSFTA Judges’ Education and Beyond
Submitted by Erin Kerfoot
Did you know judges’ education goes beyond breed seminars and hands-on workshops? I t does! In fact,
there are so many components and aspects of judges’ education that many parent clubs designate separate
sub-committees to tackle each area. Let’s briefly examine what it takes to become an AKC judge. Along
with canine anatomy courses, some of the mandatory requirements include but are not lim ited to: 1)
meeting the Component Educational Units (CEU) requirements on each breed, 2) passing the Breed
Standard Exam(s) at AKC Canine College, 3) completing six stewarding assignments, an d 4) attending
the AKC Basic Judging Institute. A parent club’s judges’ education committee is tasked with developing
resources, providing education, and promoting understanding of their breed. Through these educational
services, new judges should be fully informed, proficiently knowledgeable, and able to p ass their exams
and interviews with flying colors.
What are CEU credits and how are they earned? Judges need a minimum of 10 CEU credits to judge a
single breed. These credits come from a variety of experiences. The highest valued CEU credits come
from Judges’ Education Committee Seminar and Hands-on Workshop at a National and at Advanced In-
stitutes (there are three of these institutes: American Dog Show Judges “ADSJ”, the Dog Judges Associa-
tion of America “DJAA” and the Oregon Dog Judges “ODJ”). Additional credits come fr om webinars,
mentoring and tutoring, ringside observations, and AKC’s Canine College breed courses. The more ave-
nues and platforms of education available, the more opportunities judges have to learn about and better
understand our breed.
How is the ESSFTA Judges’ Education Committee structured? The Judges Education Co mmittee (JEC) is
comprised of a Steering Committee and Sub-Committees / Task Forces. A steering committee provides
high-level oversight and strategic guidance for a project, often with decision-making authority. The Steer-
ing Committee includes the chair and co-chair of the specific sub-committee / task force and the ESSFTA
officers and Board of Governors. A sub-committee / task force is a smaller group of members assembled
to address a specific problem or complete a focused task, project, and/or objective. Essentially, a steering
committee sets the direction while a task force does the hands-on work to achieve it. Together they play a
vital role in educating and mentoring individuals who want to become judges, know more about the Eng-
lish Springer Spaniel breed, and those who want to know more about the judging process. The committee
presents at AKC judges’ education seminars, other judges’ groups that request a program, the National
Specialty, and regional clubs that invite them to present at their specialties. They are responsible for de-
veloping educational materials, producing the ESSFTA Breed Illustrated Standard, and training the
Judges’ Education presenters and Ringside Mentors.
What are the sub-committees/task forces of the JEC? There are four JEC sub-committees/task forces cur-
rently formed to better address the all-encompassing needs of a judges’ education program. Each sub-
committee/task force will be comprised of 4 to 6 members, including the JEC Coordinator, with the op-
tion to invite subject matter experts as needed.

ESSFTA Judges’ Education and Beyond (Cont)
Submitted by Erin Kerfoot
Both the Ringside Mentoring and Tutoring and Virtual Breed Course Development and A KC’s Canine
College are currently in development. The JEC is seeking interested members to serve on those sub-
committees to help develop standardized and quantitative measures of training and evaluating mentors,
judges, and breeders. Each of the four sub-committees will focus on a specific task or com ponent of
judges’ education. With the help of the JEC Coordinator, information, resources, and materials generated
from each sub-committee will work in tandem to make a highly successful educational pr ogram for judg-
es, breeders, and ESS enthusiasts.
Who can apply to be a member of the JEC? The ESSFTA and JEC welcome qualified ind ividuals to ap-
ply to join the Judges’ Education Committee. New members will be added as current members rotate off.
The goal will be to maintain a diverse group of experts with a variety of experience and c apabilities. A
JEC member term is three years with the option to re-apply and serve multiple terms. A qualified individ-
ual will have some or all of the criteria listed on the Judges’ Education Committee Application. Qualifi-
cations include but are not limited to attendance at National Specialties, participation in and/or attendance
of JEC seminar presentations, and number of AKC champions and/or field champions bred. This is not a
strictly quantitative scoring system; rather when evaluating candidates, the committee wi ll look at all rel-
evant experience, the personal statement of interest, and the current needs of the committee for specific
skill sets. The application covers all aspects of the Judges’ Education sub-committees and task forces.
Qualified candidates not selected for an immediate assignment will be placed in a “ready now” pool
should a position become available. The JEC will do its best to assign candidates to their area of greatest
interest but is very appreciative of those who are flexible in how they are willing to serve .
Where can I apply and what is the timeline for applications and appointments? You can find the Judges’
Education Committee Application on the ESSFTA website under “Education” and then select “Judges’
Education.” Applications will be solicited in January and submitted to the JEC Coordinator by February
15th. The existing JEC will review, vet, and rank the applications. The JEC Coordinator will then submit
the committee's input to the Board of Governors and the board will make a final recommendation that
will be voted on at the ESSFTA Annual Meeting.
We are extremely appreciative of the work done by the Educational Seminar and Hands-on Workshop
and Breed Illustrated Standard sub-committees, the valuable input from members, and the enthusiasm of
all who are committed to making our breed education the very best! We look forward to receiving appli-
cations and welcoming members who bring a wealth of knowledge, experience, and know-how.

wellbeing of our breed are tied to systems by which the potential of individual dogs to serve future
generations as breeding stock is identified. Judges’ opinions are sought, and a value is placed upon
these evaluations.
Judges Education provides support and educational experiences for current and prospective judges.

es engage in an in-depth discussion of traits outlined in a Breed Standard, observation of dogs, dis-
cussion with breed experts, hands-on practice examining dogs, and writing critiques of the dogs they
examined.

Judges’ Education Committee Membership and
Application Process FAQs
Submitted by Erin Kerfoot
The Judge’s Education Committee of ESSFTA is charged with developing educational materials and delivering
presentations/workshops and written materials for current and prospective judges. These materials are also inval-
uable to those in the fancy interested in increasing their knowledge of the breed.
The ESSFTA Parent Club maintains a Judges Ed Committee and Task Forces/SubCommittees to carry out the
work needed to support these educational initiatives. We strive for committees/task forces composed of individ-
uals with diverse backgrounds and expertise.
FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Q: What does the requirement “in good standing” mean?
A: A club member is considered “in good standing” if that the individual is fully compliant with the club's rules
and regulations, has paid all required dues or fees, and is not currently facing any disciplinary acti ons, allowing
them to fully participate in the club's activities and benefits without restrictions. Further information is available at
(https://www.akc.org/clubs-
delegates/clubs/forming-a-club/form-club-glossary/ and
https://englishspringerspaniels.org/what-it-means-to-be-a-member/)
Q: How do I know if I’m a qualified individual?
A: A qualified individual will have some or all of the criteria listed on the Judges’ Ed Application ( attended Na-
tional Specialties, Attended Judges’ Ed presentations, Bred Champions, etc.). It is not a strictly quantitative scor-
ing system; rather, when evaluating candidates, we will look at all relevant experience, the personal statement of
interest, and the current needs of the committee for specific skill sets.
Q: Who and how will the decisions be made as to whom will be invited to join the committee or task force/
subcommittee?
A: Applications will be submitted to the Chairperson of the Judges’ Ed Committee, vetted by the existing com-
mittee and ranked. The Chairperson will submit the Committees input to the Board of Governors and the BOG
will make a final recommendation that will be voted on at the Annual Meeting.

Application Process FAQs
Submitted by Erin Kerfoot
Q: It seems like the same people stay on committees forever. How will you make sure you bring in fresh per-
spectives?
A: All committee members will be subject to a 3 year rotation schedule after which they will ne ed to reapply;
members up for rotation who apply for an additional 3 year term will not participate in the evaluation of candi-
dates.
In order to make sure that there is are orderly transitions, the current committee will be assigne d 3, 4, or 5 year
terms as a starting place. This will assure that we don’t have the entire committee turn over at the same time.
Q: What is the difference between the Steering Committee/ Judges Ed Committee and the Task Forces/Sub-
Committees?
A: A steering committee provides high-level oversight and strategic guidance for a project, often with decision-
making authority. The Steering Committee includes the chair and co-chair of the specific sub-committee / task
force and the ESSFTA officers and Board of Governors. A task force is a smaller group of members assembled to
address a specific problem or complete a focused task, project, and/or objective. Essentially, a steering committee
sets the direction while a task force does the hands-on work to achieve it. Together they play a vital role in
educating and assisting individuals who want to become judges or want to know
more about English Springer Spaniel judging.
Q: Why does the application ask the question: I am willing to serve on an assigned subcommittee task force
even if it is not the primary area of interest selected above?
A: We understand and appreciate that some individuals are passionate about a specific area; however, they may
also be willing to lend their expertise in other areas in service to the club. We wanted to give applicants the op-
portunity to communicate both their primary area of interest and their willingness to serve in other areas.

Have you tried Beginner Novice?
How long ago were you and your dog in the obedience ring? Obedience competition has had many
changes in the last decade. Thanks to the AKC adding many new levels of titles that make the start to
competing in the Novice classes more welcoming to trainers and dogs who are new to the sport.
Dip a toe in competition with the newer Beginner Novice class for a little more freedom to praise the
dog and build confidence before moving on to the ‘regular class’, Novice A or B.
How are these two novice classes different? The Beginner Novice (BN) requires that you and the dog
show that you perform the heel on leash, figure fight, fit for exam, sit-stay and recall. The Novice A or B
exercises include heel on leash, figure eight, stand for examination, heel off leash, recall, sit-stay
while you get your leash and the sit and down-stay group exercises.
Thank you to four great experienced obedience competitors for comparing the two quite
different classes, and sharing their training tips.
Springer trainer, Janet Kolakoski, told me, “I have started all my dogs in the beginner novice class
since the AKC began the program 14 years ago. Beginner Novice is a good introduction to obedience trial
experience for the dog. The fact that you are allowed to praise one time on heeling exercises and the
recall helps to give confidence to the dog.
“The BN sit for exam is a good introduction to the examination part of the stand for examination the
dogs will perform in the Novice ring. I feel the sit-stay in Beginner Novice is very challenging. It requires

“The AKC Beginner Novice class was designed to be a bridge from rally to obedience. It is a good way to
get rally competitors started in the obedience ring. During the heeling exercise and figure 8, you can give
a phrase of praise once during the exercise. Also, on the recall, if the dog does not come on the first
command you can give an addition command (with a point deduction) as well as a phrase of praise as
the dog is coming to you.
“In my opinion, the sit/stay walk around exercise is the hardest exercise in Beginner Novice.
“Showing your dog in Beginner Novice is a good way to get your dog comfortable in the ring. The extra
phrase of praise is an excellent way to communicate to your dog that he/she is doing a great job.
“I have noticed while judging that most exhibitors forget to give the phrase of praise during the exercis-
es. A good time to use this is when the dog is worried, out of position or distracted. The additional com-
mand, if used properly, will get the dog's attention again. On the figure 8, if the dog lags on the outside
turn, that is an excellent time to give your allowed extra phrase of praise. Make sure your extra
command is in a happy, upbeat voice.

E NGLISH SPRINGER SPANIEL FOUNDATION
Submitted by: Francie Nelson
Dear Friends of English Springer Spaniels:
On behalf of the Board of Directors of the English Springer Spaniel Foundation, I thank you for your generous and
continuing support. Since the Foundation’s inception in 1995, we have advanced the health of English Springer
Spaniels through significant investments in research, communication, and education. We hope that you will contin-
ue your support!
We are grateful to all of you who have provided invaluable support to our PRA Study, led by Dr. Keiko Miyadera,
now affiliated with Michigan State University. Breeders and exhibitors across the U.S. generously invited us into
their homes, shows, and trials, providing access to their dogs for sampling and candidly sharing their concerns.
We could not do this work without you and it is making a difference. Here are some recent highlights:
1) Publication
A peer-reviewed paper on the ESS work, authored by Dr. Miyadera, has just been published. "Veterinary Ophthalmology"
is the premier journal in the field and it will help disperse current knowledge among veterinary ophthalmologists. The
Foundation will distribute a lay summary to the Springer community in the near future.
2) AKC CHF grant
Dr. Miyadera’s work has highlighted the need for an expanded longitudinal study. With your help, we intend to support a
second phase of her work, and her grant request is currently under review by the CHF. The Foundation supported the
beginnings of a second phase study at the National Specialty, making it possible for Dr. Miyadera to continue her work.
Your support helped make it happen!
3) Dr. Miyadera at the Field Trials
The National Amateur and National Open field trials in December 2024 provided a great opportunity to add more field
dogs to the PRA study. The Foundation supported Dr. Miyadera’s travel.

hemolytic anemia (IMHA) in English Springer Spaniels. Spaniel breeds may be particularly vulnerable. Dr. Steven
