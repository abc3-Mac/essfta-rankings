The English Springer Spaniel Field Trial Association (ESSFTA) is the American Kennel Club (AKC) parent club for the breed. This
means that the ESSFTA is the steward for the breed standard as well as AKC competition standards and guidelines. The ESSFTA
also works with organizations such as the Orthopedic Foundation for Animals (OFA)the Eye Registration Foundation (CERF)
establish health and genetic testing standards and guidelines.
The ESSFTA’s activities are guided by a focus first and foremost on that which benefits the breed, and maintains the heritage
entrusted to us by generations past. We support our members in their efforts to promote, protect and enjoy this marvelous
breed, and provide information to the public to acquaint them with its history and character.
With its sister organization, the Springer Spaniel Foundation, the ESSFTA works to improve the genetic health of the English
Springer Spaniel via research projects and breeder education projects, and fosters the history of the breed via the joint
Archives committee, as well as through our relationship with the Dog Foundation.
The ESSFTA does not directly undertake rescue activities. It supports and applauds the efforts of the various groups devoted
specifically to that cause.

PRESIDENT’S MESSAGE
Message from the President
June 15, 2026
Summer Greetings to all Springer Enthusiasts!
The Annual Meeting of the ESSFTA was held May 1st and 2nd in Nashville. It was truly inspiring
to see all the progress we have made against the strategic plan and to discuss and debate our
plans for 2026/27. The new Governors and Officers were officially put in their roles – and it’s a
great group! Our new governors are Jim O’Keefe, Garry Soper and Sarah Willms. New officers
are Patty Mortara, Digital Technology, and Jennifer Sivertson , Treasurer. We also thanked
the departing Governors – Jacquie Dean, Mark Geibert, and Steve Seltzer- and Holly Boggess
who is leaving the Digital Technology role (but staying on as a Governor). We recognized Kathy
Nestor for 13 years of service as our Treasurer – she is truly heroic and we offer her our
gratitude for all those years of keeping us on track!
The BOG has continued to make progress on key initiatives – finalizing the revised Blue Book
and planning several Judging Seminars this spring and summer across the nation. Bringing in
new Field Trial judges and providing mentoring to current judges is key to the continued health
of our sport.
The Conformation/Performance Judges Ed subcommittee for the Illustrated Standard has made
significant progress and Erin Kerfoot, Judges’ Ed Chair and I.S. Committee Chair, will share
additional information in this Spotlight.
We are planning in earnest for the 2026 National Specialty in Albany, Oregon, October 10 - 17.
Many events and reservations are open and the joint committees of Puget Sound ESS,
Willamette Valley ESS, and the National Specialty are working on finalizing the remaining plans.
All current information is available on the Parent Club website:
https://englishspringerspaniels.org/2026-national-specialty/ Liz Purkaple has let us know that
she will be stepping back from her role as National Specialty Treasurer – we wish her well as
she embarks on an exciting career change. We are delighted to share that Ashley Dornak has
agreed to take on the National Specialty Treasurer role – we are very fortunate to have her in
this role.

From the English Springer Foundation:
Your Help is Needed!
Glaucoma is increased pressure within the eye. The balance of fluid production and drainage is
responsible for maintaining normal pressure within the eye. In Glaucoma, the drain becomes clogged,
but the eye keeps producing fluid. Therefore, the pressure in the eye increases, and this can actually
cause the eye to stretch and enlarge. In the UK, Glaucoma is a recognised condition in English Springers,
and Springers are one of the breeds that undergo testing and grading through gonioscopy. Given the
enormous impact of glaucoma in terms of pain and blindness, anything that improves our ability to
identify at-risk dogs would be extremely welcome. – Louise Scott & Lesley Field, Co-Chairs, UK English
Springer Spaniel Health Committee
Last fall, the Foundation received a request for support, strongly endorsed by the UK Health Committee,
from Dr. Cathryn Mellersh. Cathryn is Head of the Canine Genetics Centre (CGC), based at the University
of Cambridge in the UK, where she and her colleagues investigate the underlying genetics of hereditary
diseases that affect dogs. She asked for our support of a proposal that she ultimately submitted to the
US Canine Health Foundation requesting funds to research a genetic marker for a specific type of
Glaucoma. We agreed, by letter, to support the inclusion of English Springers among the 13 breeds that
Cathryn intends to study and to help facilitate the gathering of DNA samples here in the US. Her
proposal is moving through the CHF approval process.
It is entirely possible that Glaucoma exists in our US English Springer population; perhaps it is not
being recognized or reported as references to the disease are scant here.
Here’s how you can help. If your Springer has been diagnosed with Glaucoma, please contact
VP of Communication/International Relations, Francie Nelson, via email at
fanfarespringers@comcast.net. Just provide your contact information and your Springer’s registered
name, gender, and age when diagnosed. If we need a DNA sample and pedigree, the Foundation’s

by Sarah A. Ferrell
Tracking Down Education, Information, Shopping and Fun on the World Wide
Web
In April 2026, the Springer Fancy lost a great Spaniel man, Rob Sataloe. Rob bred beautiful, happy
dogs and spread decades of Springer devotion. He could brighten any room, any show ring and make
Springer tails wag with the sound of his voice. Rob mentored many new fanciers. I had the pleasure to
interview Rob for this article. Thank you to Rob Sataloe for the many wonderful memories of his generous
spirit and the gifts he made to our breed. Thank you, too, to Ruth Dehmel and Susan Campbell for their
help with this article.
Build More Majors:
Mentor a New Handler
“If only there were more 5 point majors!” Did you ever sit, weary and far from home in a
stark motel room, looking at your beautiful ready-to-finish dog and make that wish? One way
to increase entries is to never fail to reach out and extend help and an encouraging welcome to
new faces in the grooming area and at ringside. Extending your hand of friendship, or a short
‘how to groom for the ring’ lesson, could be just the nudge a new exhibitor needs to be hooked
on showing their dog.

Perfected Technique: Dehmel Wrote the Book
Years ago, before I set foot for the very first time into the breed ring, a smiling woman
walked over to my set-up and gave me the courage to wrestle the butterflies in my stomach
SPRINGER SPOTLIGHT 8 JUNE. 2026
=== PAGE 10 ===
Webhunter, con’t
and take my young dog into the ring. She commented that my dog looked very nice. She smiled
and asked if I would like ‘ a little grooming help on his neck area?’
You bet I did. With one deft hand lifting his coat on her steel comb and the other hand
fluttering away with thinning shears, she found the lovely arch of my dog’s neck and left me
staring with gaping astonishment. Just as our breed standard requires, my dog suddenly
transformed to have a “neck (that) is moderately long, muscular, clean and slightly arched at
the crest. It blends gradually and smoothly into sloping shoulders. “
She showed me how to properly trim his tail, demonstrating how to use the comb and the
shears, thinning until a perfect merry Springer tail appeared. Amazingly, as she trimmed, and
gently talked to my dog—who appeared to know he was in the hands of a pro and an angel of
grooming mercy—again the breed standard’s words became flesh: “The croup slopes gently to
the set of the tail, and tail-set follows the natural line of the croup. “ She taught me which way
to point the scissor tips to make “feet round or slightly oval, compact and well-arched… well-
feathered between the toes.”
This master groomer who gave me the courage to step before the judge, and who made my
dog look similar to other lovely dogs as he floated ‘round the ring was Ruth Dehmel. Dehmel is
a breeder (renditioness.net), master groomer, peerless Springer artist, dazzling jewelry
designer, and co-owner of great Westminster winner, ‘Ch. Felicity's Diamond Jim’ ( with co-
owners Teresa Patton ,Allen Patton , Diana Hadsall). Dehmel has a heart and generosity as huge
as her artistic and dog-handling talents. She is a stalwart champion and advocate for all
Springers.

As gracious as she is accomplished, Campbell shares her tool preferences and grooming
expertise. A professional clipper is a must for shaping your Springer’s coat.
Campbell prefers Andis clippers with #10, #8.5, and #5F blades. She uses a dremel tool with a
flex shaft grinder, and does not like battery operated grinders. The Mars Coat King works
better, in her opinion, than the newer Furminator tool. Her favorite straight scissor is made by
Roseline and her preferred thinning shears are Kenchii Lotus. She ferries all these tools and
products to shows in a D-Flite tack box.
http://greyhoundcomb.com/
http://www.groomerschoice.com/
When asked how to convince squirmy puppies and noise sensitive dogs to have their head
and ears trimmed with less drama, Campbell suggests, “Have a lot of background noise, i.e.
dryer or fan running nearby. More noise makes the dog not notice the clipper sounds as much.”
Wahl Arco clippers are her preference for the most quiet clipping.Campbell is upbeat and
cheerfully determined to make her dogs all that they can be.

An Upbeat Handler Creates Ring-Happy Dogs
Walk the crowded aisles of grooming set-ups at any crowded specialty show and you will
learn allot about Springer grooming. Without fail, if you find yourself beside the grooming table
of breeder, Springer artist and gentleman, Rob Satoloe, you will be greeted by a welcoming
smile and ready explanations to your grooming questions. Sataloe’s upbeat dog may be
standing on the table, paws around his neck, kissing Satoloe’s cheerful face, but undoubtedly
Satoloe will stop what he’s doing to greet you with cordial warmth. He will generously share
SPRINGER SPOTLIGHT 13 JUNE. 2026
=== PAGE 15 ===
Webhunter, con’t
grooming secrets gleaned from many years of keeping his hands on his dogs and his artist’s
pencil on his sketch pad. Check out any specialty’s auction to benefit worthy Springer causes
and you might go home with a piece of Sataloe’s precise and lovely Springer art.
Sataloe has firm opinions about the tools he favors. He uses Double K 401 cable clippers
with #15, # 10, and # 40 blades. For noise sensitive dogs he suggests a piece of cotton in the
ears to make the noisy grooming environment easier to endure. He can’t be pinned down to
just one favorite grooming product. He quips, “Only 1? Heck, if I didn’t like using lots of
products and tools, I would be into smooth coated dogs!” He orders most of his supplies from
Pet Edge and Cherrybrook.
www.petedge.com/
www.cherrybrook.com
Three to four days after a good bath, Satoloe commences three hours of focused pre-ring
grooming. Handler and dog find the time on the table relaxing as long as the groomer’s
nemesis, carpal tunnel syndrome, doesn’t kick in to remind Sataloe he has been creating
beautiful tight feet and graceful flowing furnishings for more than a few decades. Birchwood
dogs’ happy, ‘glad to meet you!’ temperaments are the reward Satoloe reaps for his rule that
time spent with dogs should be fun.

Liz Pike, ESSFTA President, summarizes the AKC report below:
AKC Statistics – Beyond the Numbers
I received registration and event participation data from AKC and my first thought was – is it time – or maybe
already past time- to worry?
I have lots of numbers and charts (and I’m happy to send them to anyone who loves numbers as much as I do!) but
for everyone, I’ll share a few key points:
• Both litter and individual dog registrations peaked in 2021 and have fallen to the lowest levels since 2012.
Individual dog registrations have fallen by 32% since 2012.
• There has also been a decrease in dogs competing in both conformation and companion events while
performance events have experienced a significant increase in participation (note that performance event
participation may be understated as the data uses “dogs placing” to generate these charts). Agility, Rally,
Obedience, and Tracking are considered Companion events. Coursing Ability, Farm Dog Certification, Fast
CAT, Field Trials, Hunting Tests and Scent Work are considered Performance events.

• Another startling observation is how few dogs/bitches have been bred – what is the impact on genetic
diversity for our breed?
So – as the Parent Club and devoted supporters of our breed – what should we be doing to encourage more
owners to register their dogs and participate in all these wonderful dog sports? How do we welcome new our
puppy buyers and introduce them to their fellow Springer enthusiasts? How can we ALL make sure that the future
of our breed is secure?
